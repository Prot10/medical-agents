## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

The case is a 38-year-old woman with MS on natalizumab whose new fatigable ptosis, diplopia, jaw and deltoid fatigue were treated as an MS relapse. The patient block no longer gives the result of the brain MRI the agent must order, nor NIF values. The pathway now reads: bedside ice-pack test (recommended, positive on the right), AChR antibody and thyroid function (required; AChR binding 4.2 nmol/L, blocking and modulating raised, MuSK negative, JCV index 3.1), RNS (required; 14% deltoid and 18% nasalis decrement, normal CMAPs, no LEMS-type increment), gadolinium brain MRI (required; no new or enhancing lesion, no PML features), SFEMG (optional), mediastinal CT with contrast (required; 2.8 cm well-defined anterior mediastinal mass most consistent with thymoma), baseline respiratory function (recommended, normal), a drug-interaction check (recommended, because MG immunosuppression on top of natalizumab raises PML risk) and literature (optional, last). The CSF JCV PCR that appeared in the blood panel without an LP is gone. Musts: pyridostigmine, cautious low-dose prednisone, azathioprine only after natalizumab is stopped and TPMT is known, thymectomy for the thymoma, a natalizumab switch planned with the MS team, and drug safety advice. MS relapse and PML are low in the differential after the MRI.
