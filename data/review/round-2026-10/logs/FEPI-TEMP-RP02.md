## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 31-year-old commercial pilot has two early-morning convulsions and weekly rising-epigastric and deja vu episodes he blamed on anxiety, and now hemoptysis, dyspnea and left calf pain after a 20-hour car trip. The patient block is unchanged. The pathway is now: labs (D-dimer 4850, levetiracetam 8.2 mcg/mL low, coagulation and renal values normal; prolactin removed), ECG (normal sinus rhythm, 96/min), CTPA (bilateral segmental emboli, small left lower lobe infarct, no right heart strain, no nodule), epilepsy-protocol MRI (normal; the implausible lung nodule on the brain MRI is gone), routine and sleep-deprived EEGs (normal apart from wicket spikes) and the 72-hour ambulatory EEG, now required, which captures three left anterior temporal-onset seizures, one evolving to bilateral tonic-clonic from sleep. The drug check is required (inducers lower DOAC and warfarin levels). FDG-PET is waste (its report answers from the fallback); two literature searches are optional, general and correctly sourced. Musts: anticoagulation for at least 3 months, a levetiracetam increase, no flying until FAA recertification, driving, SUDEP and CPAP; prohibitions on inducers, valproate and attributing the convulsions to PE syncope. The echo fallback now shows the documented mitral valve prolapse.
