## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
A 36-year-old man with juvenile myoclonic epilepsy who ran out of levetiracetam 3 days ago and slept 4 hours a night for a week arrives still convulsing 22 minutes after onset, having had 4 mg of IV lorazepam from the paramedics without effect. The pathway is core labs (levetiracetam undetectable, lactate 8.2 with bicarbonate 18, CK 1840, troponin 0.06, WBC 14.8, procalcitonin 0.08, alcohol and drug screen negative), non-contrast CT and continuous video-EEG (initial suppression, recovering reactive background, interictal generalized polyspike-wave, no electrographic seizures), with MRI, the drug check and the literature search optional or recommended. The two reviewer notes are applied: the template blood cultures are gone and are now an avoided action, with the 38.1 C explained as seizure-related hyperthermia by a partner who reports he was afebrile all day, a procalcitonin of 0.08 and a red herring on the vitals; beta-hCG is out of a male patient's panels. The post-loading drug level, the recovery EEG, the serial troponin and the lumbar-puncture follow-up are deleted, the EEG no longer names the syndrome, and the drug check no longer carries a treatment plan or valproate teratogenicity in a man. Because the history says first-line treatment was already given, the musts now start at the next tier: repeat the benzodiazepine once while loading levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (or valproate 40 mg/kg), define the refractory step, continue video-EEG until baseline, hydrate and cool without antimicrobials, and dispense levetiracetam with a refill plan, adherence and driving counseling before discharge.
