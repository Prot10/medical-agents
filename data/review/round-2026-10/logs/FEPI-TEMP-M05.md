## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

Ms. Hayashi (renamed from a duplicate) is a 38-year-old left-handed woman with three months of weekly focal impaired-awareness seizures and an outside standard MRI reporting a "possible prior small hemorrhage". The policy now requires the epilepsy-protocol MRI and the routine EEG, recommends video-EEG capture of the habitual events, and leaves ECG, neuropsychology, drug check and literature optional; the stop rule is MRI plus EEG with two extra actions allowed. The MRI reads as a dedicated 3T study with SWI and post-contrast sequences added for the lesion, showing a 14 mm right mesial temporal cavernous malformation with a complete hemosiderin rim, no edema or enhancement, and normal hippocampi. Two contradictions in the patient block were fixed (no prior imaging vs the outside MRI; examined four hours vs three days after the last event). The stray prolactin claim, the wrong lateralization claim and the unsourced figures left the key evidence. Musts: lamotrigine or levetiracetam, contraception and folic acid, HLA-B*15:02 before carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine, surgical referral, driving; prohibitions include treating the lesion as an acute hemorrhage and starting valproate. The hemorrhagic tumor mimic is now `low`. Literature cites Akers 2017, Bernasconi 2019 and Glauser 2013 correctly.
