## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 58-year-old man with a right lateral medullary infarct from a right V3 vertebral dissection 36 hours after chiropractic manipulation. The pathway now runs NCCT, labs, ECG and CTA (required), then the drug check (recommended, it informs thrombolysis), MRI with DWI and fat-saturated T1 (recommended), TTE and 24-hour Holter recorded on the stroke unit (recommended) and an optional literature output. The HPI no longer reports the CT result or argues the vestibular mimic away. The exam's HINTS now reads as a central pattern on all three components (normal head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus, skew), the nystagmus and Horner descriptions are anatomically consistent, and the NIHSS of 4 is itemised as limb ataxia 2, sensory 1, dysarthria 1. The MRI no longer places the dissection at V4 (CTA shows no intracranial extension), and the 38-hour-old intramural hematoma is described as isointense to mildly hyperintense on T1. The case now states the decision: IV thrombolysis is given (about 2 hours from onset, disabling deficit with inability to walk and dysphagia, BP 172/96, normal glucose, platelets and INR; an extracranial dissection is not a contraindication), and thrombectomy is not indicated (no LVO). The drug check now flags the lisinopril angioedema risk and the 24-hour antithrombotic pause. After the 24-hour imaging, antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy for at least 3 months (AHA/ASA 2021, COR 2a), swallow assessment before oral intake, atorvastatin 80 mg, follow-up vessel imaging and no further neck manipulation. Total cholesterol is corrected to 202 so the LDL of 128 holds, and the borderline ECG voltage is no longer called LVH.
