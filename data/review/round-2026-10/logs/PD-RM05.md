## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

This 58-year-old woman has 4 years of left-onset PD with a documented response of more than 50% to levodopa, now with diary-documented end-of-dose wearing-off 2.5 hours after each dose, OFF-period freezing and two falls; she takes prochlorperazine up to twice a month for migraine nausea and levothyroxine with omeprazole. Clinically established PD with fluctuations (G20.A2) stays. The policy is a required MRI (any protocol) and a required medication review, recommended thyroid function (TSH 5.8 mIU/L, free T4 0.9), and optional DaTscan, neuropsychological battery and counselled PD gene panel (justified by her mother's PD, not by "young onset"); the literature step is gone. The DaTscan is now a binary report with binding ratios. The medication review now names the prochlorperazine problem with its alternatives, and two real points the old output missed: omeprazole reduces levothyroxine absorption, and ondansetron is contraindicated with apomorphine. The musts are: diagnose established PD and explain why twice-monthly prochlorperazine does not cause it; stop prochlorperazine; manage the wearing-off (shorter interval, entacapone, MAO-B inhibitor or agonist with the ICD warning); physiotherapy with cueing for freezing; increase levothyroxine and recheck TSH. Prochlorperazine, hypothyroidism and MSA are now low, written as aggravating or coexisting factors where they are.
