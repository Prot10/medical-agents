## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
44-year-old man with three witnessed episodes of dysphasia building over 8-10 minutes and each followed by a migrainous headache; a GP Holter found asymptomatic paroxysmal AF and apixaban was started; he takes ibuprofen for the headaches. The HPI no longer says the speech change is sudden, and 12-15 units a week is no longer called heavy drinking. The structured assessment applies ICHD-3 (4 of 6) and records CHA2DS2-VA 0 without the episodes. Brain MRI (recommended) no longer reports an MRA; MRA head and neck is a separate recommended study (normal). The required loop recorder now actually shows the dissociation: AF only at night and a symptomatic daytime episode in sinus rhythm with no AF in the preceding 24 hours. The required drug check is apixaban against ibuprofen. Musts: explain both diagnoses, stop apixaban with cardiology (CHA2DS2-VA 0, ESC 2024), alcohol and weight reduction (Voskoboinik 2020), stop ibuprofen while anticoagulated (paracetamol or triptan instead), diary without prevention. The invented REDUCE-AF trial, GGT/ALT values and PFO on TOE are gone; cardioembolic TIA is low in the differential.
