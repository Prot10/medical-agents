## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

This 41-year-old right-handed woman with obesity has 18 months of stereotyped focal impaired-awareness seizures (burning-rubber smell, rising epigastric sensation, staring, lip-smacking, right-hand automatisms, postictal expressive language difficulty), two of them progressing to convulsions after sleep deprivation, a standard 1.5T MRI read as normal ten months ago, and a misattribution to panic disorder; she has never taken an antiseizure medication. The HPI no longer gives the EEG result or argues the answer. The epilepsy-protocol MRI the agent orders now shows the left anteroinferior temporal encephalocele through a defect of the middle cranial fossa floor at the greater sphenoid wing, with normal hippocampi and a partial empty sella (no gadolinium); the routine EEG shows left anterior temporal discharges and slowing; the recommended video-EEG captures two habitual seizures with F7/T3 onset; the recommended CT bone windows show the 5.2 mm defect. Labs (prolactin removed), ECG, FDG-PET, neuropsychology, the drug check (ibuprofen, stopped sertraline, sulfonamide allergy excluding zonisamide) and a general, correctly sourced literature search are optional. Management: lamotrigine or levetiracetam first line, contraception and folic acid, epilepsy surgery evaluation of the encephalocele (not "drug-resistant"), neuro-ophthalmology assessment of the blurred discs, pulsatile tinnitus and empty sella before any skull-base repair, driving and SUDEP counselling; valproate and zonisamide prohibited. Lateralization claims now rest on EEG and MRI concordance and postictal language, not on the right-hand automatisms. The differential is graded after the work-up.
