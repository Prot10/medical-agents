## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 63-year-old right-handed man, 2 months after HSV-1 encephalitis, has a week of paranoia, aggression, insomnia, incoherent paraphasic speech, a right-arm focal seizure, orofacial and choreiform dyskinesias and labile blood pressure with diaphoresis: post-HSV anti-NMDAR encephalitis (CSF anti-GluN1 1:32, serum 1:10, CSF HSV PCR negative). The prior HSV scar is now left frontotemporal, and the EEGs left-lateralised, to match his aphasia, right-sided deficit and right-arm seizure; the MRI shows no new lesion, diffusion restriction, enhancement or haemorrhage. CSF glucose was raised to a normal ratio (0.62). The serum labs no longer contain the CSF antibody, and list serum anti-GluN1, neuronal-surface and onconeural panels, CK and toxicology; the contrast CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is negative. Literature and drug checks are optional; the literature output now cites real sources correctly (Armangue 2018: 27%, 64% NMDAR). Musts: restart acyclovir until PCR excludes relapse, steroids with IVIG or PLEX (glucose monitoring, slow IVIG for CAD), no further tumour imaging, seizure treatment preferring lacosamide over escalating levetiracetam because of its behavioural effects, cEEG, ICU, rituximab/cyclophosphamide escalation, lorazepam, neurology with psychiatry; no testicular ultrasound or urology consult. The differential is all low with the evidence that excludes each mimic, including HSV relapse, structural epilepsy and levetiracetam toxicity.
