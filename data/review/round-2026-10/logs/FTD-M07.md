## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 62-year-old former financial advisor with two years of disinhibition (unauthorised trades, a $30,000 investment, rage), loss of empathy and perseveration, heavy drinking, cerebellar signs and snout/palmomental reflexes. The structured assessment now scores three Rascovsky features from the wife's history (escalating alcohol is deliberately not counted as dietary change, because drinking is the competing explanation) and returns the MoCA 20/30, which is no longer printed in the exam. Required work-up: assessment, neuropsychology (executive and social-cognition deficits, preserved recognition and construction), a complete reversible-cause panel (macrocytosis and raised GGT/transaminases only), and MRI (disproportionate bilateral frontal atrophy, superior vermian atrophy, Fazekas 2, normal mammillary bodies) reported without attributing findings to alcohol. FDG-PET (or perfusion SPECT, now authored) and genetics are optional; literature is optional and last with general, real sources. The musts now ask for the Rascovsky certainty level, treatment of alcohol and untreated sleep apnoea with reassessment after abstinence, parenteral thiamine before glucose (ataxia plus cognitive impairment meets two Caine criteria), driving and financial safety, caregiver support without ChEI/memantine, and counselling before genetic testing; disulfiram is prohibited. The differential is the assessment-time view (alcohol as sole cause moderate; Wernicke-Korsakoff, late-onset bipolar, AD and vascular low).
