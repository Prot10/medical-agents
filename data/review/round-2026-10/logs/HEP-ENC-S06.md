## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

The policy now requires the core laboratory work-up with thiamine and toxicology, a diagnostic paracentesis (ascitic PMN 142 cells/mm3, no SBP) and a non-contrast head CT (found down alone, comatose and coagulopathic, so possible head trauma; CT normal apart from atrophy), and recommends blood and urine cultures as an infection screen (afebrile, WBC 9.8), the EEG and a medication review. The literature search, the duplicate labs paracentesis, the thiamine-response panel, the serial ammonia with 72-hour GCS, the CIWA-Ar follow-up and the transplant panel are gone. The exam now matches GCS 8 (E2V2M4: withdrawal, not localization) and no longer reports asterixis or disorientation that cannot be tested in coma; the HPI no longer gives the ethanol and ammonia results. The label reads West Haven grade IV, Child-Pugh C (15), MELD-Na 36, with hypokalemia and hypoglycemia as co-precipitants; "MELD 24" is dated to the admission three months ago. Labs: the "Exclusion studies" block is replaced by lactate 1.9 and CRP 14, acetaminophen (not detected) and CK 264 are added, TSH 1.4 and zinc 54 replace the template values. The CT loses the basal ganglia hypodensity, the EEG loses the burst suppression and states reactivity and the absence of seizures, and the cultures report one time point each. The drug check flags sertraline in Child-Pugh C (hyponatremia, platelets 48), the diuretics and the benzodiazepine choice. Musts: intubation for airway protection, precipitant treatment with an infection screen, thiamine 200 mg IV TID before or with the dextrose, lactulose to 2-3 stools by NG or enema, rifaximin, potassium to 4.0 and magnesium with ECG monitoring and diuretics held, withdrawal monitoring adapted to coma, hold sertraline and review propranolol, large-volume paracentesis with albumin, primary SBP prophylaxis, assessment for superimposed alcohol-related hepatitis, enteral nutrition, transplant referral with addiction services. Prohibitions: scheduled or long-acting benzodiazepines, opioids and sedatives, attributing the coma to intoxication, protein restriction.

## Open questions for Andrea (judgment calls you could not settle)

- CT is required here although the plan lists S06 as optional: an unwitnessed collapse in an intoxicated, comatose patient with INR 3.4 and platelets 48 is possible head trauma (an ACG imaging trigger), and the case's own differential raised subdural hematoma. Revert to optional if you want strict plan uniformity.
- The drug check is recommended rather than the plan's optional: sertraline is not recommended by its label in severe hepatic impairment and adds to the hyponatremia (Na 128) and bleeding risk (platelets 48, varices), and the benzodiazepine choice for the coming withdrawal is a hepatic-dosing decision. Ruling 6 would also allow required.
- West Haven grade IV is kept with GCS 8 (E2V2M4). By the strict AASLD 2014 operative criterion, grade IV means no response even to painful stimuli, and this patient opens his eyes and withdraws to pain, which is the grade III-IV border. GCS 8 with no comprehensible speech and no command-following is conventionally coma, and Child-Pugh is C either way; if you prefer the strict criterion, either relabel grade III or make the exam E1 (GCS 7).
- New values are invented as C9 instructs and carry no diagnostic weight: lactate 1.9, CRP 14, TSH 1.4, zinc 54, CK 264, acetaminophen not detected, ascitic albumin 0.2 g/dL (derived from the existing SAAG 1.6), and the fallback ECG.
- The alcohol-related hepatitis must and differential entry are new: bilirubin 18.4 mg/dL and AST 488 U/L after heavy drinking make a superimposed severe alcohol-related hepatitis a question a hepatologist would raise. Delete them if the case should stay a pure HE precipitant case.
