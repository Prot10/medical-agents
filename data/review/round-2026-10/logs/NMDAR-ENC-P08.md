## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 38-year-old nurse practitioner with Hashimoto thyroiditis and a 12-day encephalitis: the work-up now reads serum labs, contrast MRI, CSF, routine EEG and pelvic ultrasound (all required, MRI before LP as a soft constraint), then continuous EEG (recommended), with the interaction check and a Hashimoto literature search optional and last. The CSF report now gives its own final result (anti-GluN1 positive 1:64, CSF anti-LGI1 negative) instead of "pending", and the labs carry only the serum titre (1:160) in a regrouped report without the "exclusion studies" text; normal hepatic, toxicology, CK, AMPA, onconeural and beta-hCG results were added to complete the panel set. The arm jerking and facial weakness are now left-sided to match the right temporal MRI and EEG. The EEG impression is in ACNS format without management; a new 24-hour cEEG shows three right temporal seizures. The ultrasound describes a 5.2 cm right ovarian mature cystic teratoma with characteristic features. The ECG is sinus tachycardia at 117 and the Holter fallback shows tachycardia to 124. Musts: aciclovir until PCR negative, methylprednisolone with IVIG or plasma exchange, removal of the right ovarian teratoma, levetiracetam or lacosamide (no valproate), cEEG, ICU for dysautonomia and seizures, rituximab/cyclophosphamide escalation at 10-14 days, lorazepam for agitation, and the team. The differential adds Hashimoto encephalopathy (low) and puts every alternative at low with the case's own excluding data; a third red herring names the anti-TPO trap.
