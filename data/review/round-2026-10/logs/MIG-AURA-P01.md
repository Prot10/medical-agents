## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
52-year-old postmenopausal woman with three stereotyped episodes of expressive dysphasia that build up over 5-8 minutes, last 15-25 minutes and are each followed by a left-sided migrainous headache; GP started aspirin for a TIA query and atorvastatin; normal examination. The HPI no longer says the dysphasia starts "suddenly", and her blood pressure is consistently borderline (untreated). The structured assessment applies ICHD-3 explicitly (aphasia counts as unilateral; 4 of 6 C characteristics) and names the red flags (onset after 50, isolated aphasic aura). Brain MRI is required and no longer contains an MRA; MRA of the head and neck is a separate recommended study (normal); ESR/CRP is recommended with real values and lipids for the statin review. Musts: explain migraine with aphasic aura, stop the aspirin, review the statin by QRISK3, NSAID at headache onset with a triptan only after cardiovascular evaluation, diary without prevention. The wrong HRT prohibition is gone; TIA and GCA are low in the differential with their excluding results; the CT fallback no longer reports a granuloma the MRI does not show.
