## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

The case remains a 41-year-old smoker with a painful left Horner syndrome from a left cervical ICA dissection, NIHSS 0 and no infarct. The work-up now reads NCCT, admission bloods, ECG, CTA head and neck (required), MRI with DWI, fat-saturated T1 and MRA (recommended, because only DWI can show that there is no infarct), the drug check (required: sumatriptan and ibuprofen), then optional echocardiogram, rhythm monitoring and literature. The thrombophilia panel and the connective-tissue literature output are gone. The CTA impression now opens with the dissection, and the CTA is marked as contrast-enhanced. The MRI describes the intramural hematoma as acute (isointense to mildly hyperintense on fat-saturated T1 at 3 hours) and its impression names the dissection. The NCCT reads as a plain report with ASPECTS 10. The exam no longer says that apraclonidine testing is pending; a pearl explains why it is unnecessary once imaging shows the dissection, and another explains why facial sweating is preserved. Musts are no longer the stroke-thrombolysis template: they are antithrombotic therapy for at least 3 months (aspirin or anticoagulation, not both), stopping triptans, ergots and ibuprofen, observation, repeat vessel imaging at 3 to 6 months, smoking cessation, and follow-up of LDL and TSH. The cluster headache prohibition gives the reviewer's discriminators. The differential adds SAH and a preganglionic Horner syndrome, and all alternatives are low or very low after the work-up.
