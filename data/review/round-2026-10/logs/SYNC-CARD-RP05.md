## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

The 42-year-old woman with convulsive syncope on standing, bilateral anterior uveitis, cervical nodes, dry cough, arthralgia and an aunt with sarcoidosis now has six required steps: ECG (complete AV block, junctional escape at 38 bpm with RBBB, QRS 128 ms), targeted labs with calcium or a BMP (calcium 10.4 mg/dL at the upper limit, normal ACE as the trap, TSH 6.4 with normal free T4 consistent with her untreated subclinical hypothyroidism), TTE (LVEF 66%, basal anteroseptal echogenicity and hypokinesis), inpatient telemetry (persistent complete AV block, two NSVT runs up to 8 beats, one 4.8 s pause), cardiac FDG-PET (patchy septal uptake, intense hilar and paratracheal nodes) and EBUS-TBNA (non-necrotising granulomas, negative AFB, fungal and TB PCR tests, no lymphoma). Chest imaging (bilateral hilar and paratracheal nodes), cardiac MRI (septal mid-wall and subepicardial LGE with oedema, LVEF 61%) and a review of vitamin D, prednisone and albuterol are recommended; the literature lookup is optional. Brain MRI, CSF, head CT, EEG, tilt and exercise test are waste. The musts are: cardiac sarcoidosis by the HRS 2014 clinical criteria, monitored bed with temporary pacing as needed, a dual-chamber ICD this admission without waiting for a steroid response, corticosteroids after tissue with a steroid-sparing agent considered, repeat FDG-PET at 3 to 6 months, stopping vitamin D pending calcium and vitamin D metabolites, ophthalmology and pulmonary function, and driving advice. Conduction-system degeneration, other myocarditis, Lyme, tuberculosis, lymphoma, epilepsy, reflex syncope and thyroid disease are low or very low. The patient block changed only to remove contradictions (regular rhythm, presyncope history) and an optic disc finding that would have indicated brain imaging.

## Open questions for Andrea (judgment calls you could not settle)

None needs information only you have. Three decisions depart from the plan; I made them for clinical reasons, listed here so the lead can check them:
- Plan C pins `[calcium, ACE]`. I pinned calcium only (or a BMP, which includes it). Serum ACE has poor sensitivity and specificity and does not change management here, and the stored panel returns ACE for any lab order, so the normal-ACE trap still reaches every agent. Pinning ACE would only force a low-value test, and an exact `[calcium, ACE]` match would also fail a correct `[BMP, ACE]` order.
- The plan's tier table says "FDG-PET required instead" of CMR. I kept FDG-PET required and added CMR as recommended, not as a replacement. The echo report itself recommends CMR, without a stored CMR the case answered that order with a normal library study, and LGE extent informs the ICD decision (HRS 2014). If the lead wants CMR optional, only `importance` changes.
- Chest imaging (recommended) is a criterion the plan does not list. A patient with cough, dyspnoea and uveitis gets chest imaging before EBUS in practice, and without stored chest reports every chest study denied the lymphadenopathy.
