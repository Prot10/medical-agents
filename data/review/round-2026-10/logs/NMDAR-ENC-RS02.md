## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 50-year-old postmenopausal woman, four weeks after COVID-19, with behavioural change, mutism, seizures, orolingual dyskinesias, dysautonomia, quadriparesis with Babinski signs, ataxia, dysphagia and urinary retention: anti-NMDAR encephalitis (CSF 1:1280, serum 1:640) with an atypical MRI (symmetric dentate, cerebellar peduncle and corticospinal tract lesions with restricted diffusion). The corticospinal lesion is now right-predominant into the right thalamus to explain her left facial weakness and left hemisensory loss. Tumour screening is now a contrast CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis (negative) instead of a pelvic ultrasound. She is on valproate, enoxaparin and esomeprazole from the outside hospital (listed now); labs add a valproate level, ammonia, MOG and aquaporin-4 antibodies (negative) and onconeural antibodies. A 24-hour cEEG exists (nine right temporal seizures). Musts: acyclovir, IVIG or PLEX now plus repeat steroid (three pulses gave only transient benefit), add levetiracetam or lacosamide, cEEG, ICU, nil by mouth until swallow assessment, empirical thiamine, LP at least 12 hours after enoxaparin, escalation, lorazepam. The differential is all low and now includes ADEM/MOGAD/NMOSD, Wernicke encephalopathy and valproate encephalopathy.
