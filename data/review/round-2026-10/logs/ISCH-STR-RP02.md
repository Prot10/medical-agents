## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 35-year-old woman at 18 weeks of gestation, with chronic hypertension, migraine with aura and TTP in a previous pregnancy, had 30 minutes of left hemiparesis during exertion. Mild left lower facial weakness, dysarthria and right retro-orbital headache followed. She is now at NIHSS 2 (facial palsy 1, dysarthria 1). Neglect and extinction are now tested and absent. The occlusion moved from M1 to a right M2 superior division branch, which fits the small insular-opercular infarct and the minor deficit. The dissection now begins about 2 cm above the bifurcation and spares the bulb, with an acute (T1 isointense to mildly hyperintense) intramural hematoma. The CTA step is gone: MRI without gadolinium, with TOF MRA, fat-saturated T1 and MR venography, is the required vessel and tissue study. An optional separate MRA order returns the same findings. The decision is explicit: no IV thrombolysis (non-disabling), no thrombectomy (medium vessel, non-disabling). Low-dose aspirin is given (also pre-eclampsia prophylaxis), with enoxaparin as the alternative; there is no statin, warfarin or DOAC in pregnancy. The template thrombophilia panel is replaced by a TTP and antiphospholipid screen. It shows no active TTP (normal platelets, smear, LDH and haptoglobin) but ADAMTS13 15% with an inhibitor, so haematology plans for relapse. Antiphospholipid antibodies are negative. The NCCT now reports the old left MCA infarct and carries no management text. The echo is a real normal TTE report. The Holter is typed as 48 hours. The literature output cites ESO 2022, CADISS and TREAT-CAD correctly and is optional. The differential is updated to the assessment-time view.
