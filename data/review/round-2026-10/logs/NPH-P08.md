## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 71-year-old man with 18 months of magnetic gait, six falls, urgency incontinence and forgetfulness. The patient block no longer names NPH or a tap trial, and no longer quotes the MRI measurements, TUG or MoCA. The work-up reads: brain MRI (Evans 0.41, DESH, callosal angle 68 degrees, temporal horns enlarged, aqueductal flow void, no obstruction, Fazekas 2, MTA 1), optional labs (normal), baseline timed gait (TUG 31 s, 10-m walk 26 s, turn in 13 steps), optional MoCA (21/30, executive, attention and abstraction errors, recall 1/5 improving to 4/5 with cues), then the tap test (opening pressure 14 cmH2O, bland CSF, 45 mL removed, TUG 30 s at 2 hours and 31 s at 4 and 24 hours, 10-m walk and MoCA unchanged). The 24-hour values are new and unchanged. Next come the post-tap reassessment and, on a second CSF call, extended lumbar drainage (about 420 mL over 72 hours at 10 mL/hour clamped overnight; TUG 31 to 19 s, 39%, on day 3; 10-m walk 26 to 16 s; MoCA 21 to 24). The reports give figures only, with no "negative", "positive" or "shunt-responsive". Musts: go on to extended drainage after the single negative tap (sensitivity 26-61% for the tap against 50-100% for drainage, Marmarou 2005); shunt referral with a programmable valve on the drainage response; falls prevention. Prohibitions: no iNPH from ventriculomegaly alone; do not exclude shunt-responsive iNPH on a single negative tap; do not blame the knees. Differential: VCI moderate (Fazekas 2); everything else low. The literature step is gone.
