## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
GBS-RS17 is a severe AIDP in a 67-year-old diabetic woman who presented with community-acquired pneumonia, fever, confusion, neck stiffness and asymmetric facial and arm signs, and was intubated on Day 1. The patient block no longer gives the CSF result. It dates every exam finding and lists the current antibiotics, with home lisinopril and metformin held. The case now answers each red flag. The pre-intubation respiratory values (FVC 0.9 L, 13.2 mL/kg; PaCO2 61) explain the decline in consciousness. The CSF is acellular with protein 118 and negative Gram stain, culture, bacterial PCR panel and HSV PCR. A new required brain MRI is normal apart from chronic small vessel change, and a new recommended EEG shows diffuse reactive slowing only. The face is bilaterally weak, worse on the left, with full eye movements and flexor plantars. Three red herrings carry these resolutions. Mycoplasma IgM and the ganglioside panel are gone, and the label names the pneumonia, not an organism. Telemetry covers the first 24 h with no treatment effect. The interaction check is required (azithromycin QT, IVIg nephrotoxicity with acyclovir and vancomycin, ACE inhibitor before PE). The musts cover IVIg 2 g/kg, ICU ventilation and weaning criteria, telemetry, stopping the empirical meningitis cover once CSF and MRI are negative, doxycycline in place of QT-prolonging antibiotics, glucose control and DVT prophylaxis.
