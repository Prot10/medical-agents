## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

This 62-year-old man has two syncopes on standing that an outside cardiologist attributed to known WPW, 18 months of motor slowing, PSG-confirmed RBD, constipation, erectile dysfunction, hyposmia on Sniffin' Sticks (6/12) and a MoCA of 24/30. His examination read as MSA-P (symmetric, no decrement, no rest tremor, wide-based, putaminal MRI changes), against the PD label; it now reads as PD: left-predominant bradykinesia with decrement, an intermittent left-hand rest tremor, a narrow-based gait, a normal pull test and a normal-for-age MRI. The measured orthostatic fall (32/20 mmHg at 3 minutes, heart-rate rise 6/min) is kept as one red flag, counterbalanced by two supportive criteria (rest tremor, validated hyposmia): clinically probable PD with PD-MCI. The policy is a required MRI, a recommended 12-lead ECG (pre-excitation persists), a recommended 24-hour Holter (symptomatic events in sinus rhythm) and a recommended medication review (tamsulosin, amlodipine, clonazepam), with optional echo, MIBG (reduced) and neuropsychological battery; the literature step is gone; DaTscan, autonomic and tilt testing are waste. The musts are: diagnose probable PD with PD-MCI; attribute the syncope to orthostatic hypotension and ask cardiology to risk-stratify the pathway separately; treat the orthostatic hypotension (drug review, fluids, bed tilt, compression, then midodrine or fludrocortisone with supine monitoring); start levodopa with lying and standing BP at each step; review clonazepam.
