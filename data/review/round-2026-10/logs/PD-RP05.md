## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

This 77-year-old woman with hypertension, diabetes, a remote right hemispheric TIA and Fazekas 2 white matter change was referred for possible vascular parkinsonism, but has a 2-year insidious, left-predominant, upper-limb parkinsonism with a pill-rolling rest tremor, preceded by smell loss and constipation and accompanied by dream enactment. The policy is now MRI (required, any protocol) and DaTscan (recommended, because it answers the referral question), with optional video-PSG and an optional medication review; the literature step is gone and the drug check is no longer required (no dopamine blocker on the list). The MRI now places the absent nigrosome-1 on the right (it was on the left, ipsilateral to the signs) and no longer argues the differential. The DaTscan is a binary report (right putamen 0.88, left 1.42). The PSG is now a full report (RSWA 36%, no sleep apnoea), and a neuropsychological battery matching MoCA 26 is stored. The diagnosis is clinically probable PD (rest tremor is the only supportive criterion; the smell loss is informal), not "established". The musts are: probable PD; white matter change as coexisting small-vessel disease; levodopa start with lying and standing BP at each step (amlodipine, hydrochlorothiazide); secondary vascular prevention; physiotherapy and bedroom safety. Vascular parkinsonism is now low. The dream-enactment history was harmonised between HPI and exam, and docusate was added to the medication list.
