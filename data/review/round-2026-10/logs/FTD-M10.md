## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 64-year-old retired accountant with two years of loss of familiar-face and person knowledge (ophthalmology normal), together with apathy, loss of empathy and rigid routines. A psychiatrist diagnosed depression; she denies low mood and did not respond to sertraline. The required pathway is unchanged: informant-based assessment (3 Rascovsky features; MoCA 20/30, now returned by the assessment rather than printed in the exam), neuropsychology, the reversible-cause blood panel (normal) and MRI (severe right-predominant anterior temporal and amygdala atrophy, posterior sparing). The neuropsychology now shows normal face matching (Benton 42/54) with failed identification of famous and familiar faces, so its "associative, not perceptual" conclusion follows from the scores. FDG-PET or perfusion SPECT and literature are optional. The sertraline-discontinuation must is replaced by a behavioural plan and caregiver support that review the antidepressant rather than escalate it. Two musts were added: safety and capacity, and associative prosopagnosia with no further ophthalmology. AD and svPPA are rated low, and the amyloid claims are removed from the evidence and the notes.

## Open questions for Andrea (judgment calls you could not settle)

- Two neuropsychology values were changed to make the report consistent with its own conclusion: Benton 30/54 became 42/54, and famous faces 9/30 went from "30th percentile" to below the 5th percentile. In the right temporal variant, prosopagnosia is associative and face matching is usually preserved or only mildly reduced. Without the change, a severely impaired Benton contradicts the "intact visuospatial processing" conclusion. If you prefer a mildly reduced Benton (for example 38/54, borderline), only that value needs to change.
- Plan wording: "delete the sertraline must or reword it as a behavioural plan without a drug name". The must now says to review the antidepressant against its effect on the compulsive routines rather than escalate it. SSRIs have modest evidence for FTD compulsions, so stopping it is not clearly right.
