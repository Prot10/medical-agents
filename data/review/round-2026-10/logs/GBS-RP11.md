## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

The case stays checkpoint inhibitor-related GBS in a 70-year-old woman with stage IVB uterine cancer on pembrolizumab and lenvatinib (weight 72 kg). The patient block no longer gives TSH or spirometry and has no editorial sentences. The work-up now reads NCS/EMG (demyelinating, no RNS decrement, no myopathic units), CSF with cytology (protein 88 mg/dL, 6 lymphocytic cells/mm3, cytology negative), a contrast spine MRI for urinary retention at onset (no compression, metastasis or nodular leptomeningeal disease), baseline bloods with CK 286 and a normal troponin, AChR/MuSK antibodies (negative), respiratory function (FVC 1.8 L, 60% predicted, 25 mL/kg; MIP -28; MEP 44; 10% fall over 6 h; weak cough), 24-hour telemetry, ECG and a required interaction check on pembrolizumab and lenvatinib. The assessment admits her to ICU-level monitoring with an elective-intubation plan, stops pembrolizumab permanently and holds lenvatinib, and gives IVIg or plasma exchange with IV methylprednisolone per ASCO 2021 and ESMO 2022 (named as the governing guidance over the general EAN/PNS advice against steroids), with swallow, telemetry, overlap-toxicity screening and DVT prophylaxis. Checkpoint inhibitor myasthenia, myositis and leptomeningeal metastasis are now low with their reasons; TSH 14 with normal free T4 and urinary retention are red herrings.
