## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
A 67-year-old woman under investigation for rapidly progressive dementia arrives unresponsive 90 minutes after her first convulsion, with irregular arm jerks; her son brings an outside EEG report calling the periodic complexes the typical prion pattern. The CSF biomarkers that previously sat inside the serum labs (14-3-3, RT-QuIC, total tau 12840) and the CSF HSV PCR now live only in the lumbar-puncture report, so the diagnosis is no longer handed over with the first blood draw, and the prior MRI is history without naming its finding. The pathway is core labs (post-ictal lactate 3.4 and CK 412, normal electrolytes and magnesium, negative toxicology, negative serum LGI1, CASPR2, NMDAR and GABA-B), non-contrast CT (atrophy, no acute lesion), continuous video-EEG and MRI, then a recommended LP and drug check, with literature optional. The EEG now describes rather than diagnoses: invariant generalized periodic sharp wave complexes at about 1 Hz plus recurrent left temporal electrographic seizures with evolution, and arm jerks that are not time-locked to either, which is the point of the case. Blood cultures, the post-treatment EEG, the post-loading levetiracetam level with its ECG panel inside a lab report, and the palliative-care "literature" consult are gone. Musts give lorazepam and levetiracetam for the focal seizures, 24-hour cEEG, potassium, magnesium and an ECG because quetiapine and escitalopram both prolong the QT interval, completion of mimic exclusion, a goals-of-care discussion with palliative care, and prion-specific handling precautions; treating the periodic complexes as seizures and escalating to anaesthesia without that discussion are prohibited.
