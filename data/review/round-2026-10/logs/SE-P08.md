## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing
A 31-year-old pharmacy technician on isoniazid for latent tuberculosis is in convulsive status epilepticus that has continued through lorazepam 8 mg and a fosphenytoin load; a levetiracetam load is infusing. Forty-five minutes after arrival her roommate finds an opened stock bottle of isoniazid 300 mg tablets, about 35 tablets (10.5 g) missing, circumstances unknown. The core labs show a severe high anion gap lactic acidosis (pH 6.96, bicarbonate 8, anion gap 30, lactate 16.8, pCO2 38 on the ventilator) with a normal osmolal gap, no salicylate, acetaminophen or ethanol, a negative drug screen, CK 1840 and a negative beta-hCG; CT is normal; the cEEG shows continuous generalized spike-and-wave (electrographic status epilepticus). The isoniazid and pyridoxine levels (reference-laboratory tests), the post-pyridoxine labs and EEG, the bicarbonate "lab" criterion and the orphan panel are gone; the optional CSF is normal and now has a coherent glucose ratio and a CT-first constraint; the drug check is recommended and lists the isoniazid-phenytoin interaction; literature is optional. Musts: IV pyridoxine gram for gram with a benzodiazepine, anaesthetic infusion under cEEG if seizures continue, ventilation and hemodynamic support with bicarbonate reserved, poison center input with charcoal and hemodialysis criteria, repeat CK and liver tests, and a psychiatric assessment of the ingestion with a different latent TB regimen. Prohibited: escalating without pyridoxine, more phenytoin, waiting for a level.
