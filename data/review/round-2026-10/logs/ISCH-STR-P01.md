## Changes, in one paragraph a physician can read before re-reviewing

A 35-year-old man with a left PICA cerebellar infarct from a left V3 vertebral dissection (neck pain while cycling 3 days earlier), a known PFO and an aspirin allergy. The exam is now coherent: head impulse normal bilaterally, gaze-evoked nystagmus that changes direction, no skew, so HINTS is central; oscillopsia rather than an unexplained horizontal diplopia; NIHSS 2 (limb ataxia 2). The case now states the decision: IV thrombolysis is given (1 hour 15 minutes from onset, disabling gait ataxia; the dissection ends at the dural ring without intradural extension, so it is not a contraindication), and no thrombectomy (no LVO). The conflicting factor V Leiden results are gone with the whole thrombophilia work-up, which is not indicated. The PFO with atrial septal aneurysm is in the differential as paradoxical embolism (low) and is set aside, with no closure, because the dissection is a determined cause. After the 24-hour imaging he receives clopidogrel (aspirin allergy) or anticoagulation for at least 3 months; sumatriptan is stopped and ibuprofen avoided, and the drug check now carries these points. Labs no longer contain an unexplained sodium of 129, and Hct and cholesterol agree with the other values. The NCCT template text is gone, and the CTA reports contrast. The Holter is a 24-hour stroke-unit recording, and the TTE reads as a real report. Pathway: NCCT, labs, ECG, CTA and drug check required; MRI, TTE and Holter recommended; literature optional and last.
