## Changes
The primary diagnosis now names the active disease the management treats: cardiac syncope due to intermittent complete heart block from recurrent acute rheumatic fever with active carditis, on chronic rheumatic heart disease (ICD I44.2 kept). Recurrent rheumatic fever was removed from the differential, and its evidence moved to the key findings. The Jones reasoning was corrected. The patient is now stated to have always lived in Ghana, a high-risk population, so polyarthralgia counts as a major criterion. Fever 38.2 C and ESR 48 are the minor criteria. CRP 18.4 mg/L (1.84 mg/dL) is reported as below the 3.0 mg/dL threshold and no longer cited as support. Active carditis and ASO 340 complete the recurrence criteria. The telemetry and exercise reports now use the standard AV block terms: Mobitz II only for single dropped beats, with "2:1" and "high-grade (3:1)" block named separately. A 3.2 s recovery pause is now called high-grade block, because 2:1 conduction cannot produce it. The exercise test, and the stored exercise echo, are labelled harmful: active carditis and symptomatic paroxysmal complete heart block are absolute contraindications. The EP study and bubble study are labelled waste. Infective endocarditis with perivalvular extension was added to the differential as low likelihood, because fever, valve disease and new block raise it and the echo shows no vegetation or abscess. For the same reason a TEE order was deliberately left unpenalised. The driving must now follows the ESC 2018 Practical Instructions (Group 1 one week after a pacemaker, Group 2 once device function is confirmed), with the no-competitive-sport advice kept. Bare "acute rheumatic fever" answers will score once the central synonym request is applied. The perfect-agent check passes.
