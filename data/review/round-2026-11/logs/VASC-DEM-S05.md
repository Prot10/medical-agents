## Changes
The diagnosis is unchanged. Apixaban is no longer mandatory after the ablation of typical atrial flutter. One must now asks for an individual reassessment: establish from the ablation record and ECGs whether atrial fibrillation was ever documented, and weigh his stroke risk (CHA2DS2-VA 3) and the risk of AF after flutter ablation against his bleeding risk (age 82, falls, deep microbleeds, alcohol). If apixaban continues, 5 mg twice daily is the correct dose. The prohibition now forbids stopping apixaban solely because of falls or a few deep microbleeds. The key evidence, red herring, drug-check evidence and teaching point follow, and prolonged rhythm monitoring is no longer labelled waste, since it can inform that decision. The MRI now names all seven lacunes (the left caudate head was missing). Timed gait testing is recommended, not required (stop rule: screen, labs, MRI). Ordering perfusion SPECT, a levodopa challenge, cardiac MRI, DTI or chest CTA is labelled waste; the stored reports are kept.
