## Changes
The diagnosis label is now "Behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD), probable (provisional until reassessment after abstinence and sleep apnoea treatment), with concurrent alcohol use disorder and alcohol-related cerebellar degeneration; family history suggestive but unconfirmed" (no longer "sporadic"; ICD G31.09 and scoring unchanged), and "wernicke" is no longer a rejected answer, so a qualified answer that keeps Wernicke encephalopathy or an alcohol contribution open is not penalised. Must 0 holds probable certainty provisionally because heavy drinking and untreated sleep apnoea can also cause frontal deficits and atrophy. The thiamine must gives 200 mg three times daily, preferably IV, before or together with glucose wherever feasible and never delaying glucose for hypoglycaemia, then oral thiamine and addiction-service referral for dependence assessment and medically assisted withdrawal. A new must asks for follow-up of the raised transaminases, GGT and macrocytosis as alcohol-related liver injury (repeat after abstinence, fibrosis assessment per NICE NG50); those lab rows and the %CDT row now carry a clinical significance line. Differential 0, prohibition 5 and the MRI criterion rationale no longer present alcohol or Wernicke as excluded. The performance-status report was rewritten from the history (Karnofsky 70, ECOG 1 kept) and the head CT waste rationale now says the CT duplicates an available MRI.
Verification pass 2: the family history no longer suggests FTD as the mother's or uncle's diagnosis (the type was never established), and the MRI, FDG-PET and SPECT impressions now add that heavy alcohol use can also cause frontal atrophy or hypometabolism, so the pattern alone does not establish the cause.
Verification pass 7: the exam now grades the tandem gait as markedly impaired, matching the gait and balance report (unable beyond 3 steps), and the HPI states the mother's dementia from her late 50s (type never established) and the uncle's psychiatric institutionalisation in his 50s as facts, as the family history list does.
