## Changes
The emergency head CT is no longer required: it is optional (acceptable if MRI cannot be obtained promptly), it left the stop rule and the CT-before-MRI constraint is gone, while MRI stays required. Urine toxicology with blood alcohol is recommended instead of required and also left the stop rule; the musts, key evidence, intoxication differential, red herring and teaching notes now say that no toxidrome, undetectable blood alcohol and the 18-month course make intoxication very unlikely and that a urine immunoassay does not detect every substance, rather than that a negative screen excludes it. The bedside assessment now counts 5 of 6 Rascovsky features: the executive-predominant profile is not established at the bedside because the MoCA could not be completed, and the battery confirms it, so the diagnosis is unchanged. The stored toxicology follow-up no longer lists "BAL" (bronchoalveolar lavage) as one of its parameters, so a lavage order no longer receives it, while a toxicology order still returns the blood alcohol. The MRI rationale no longer says CT cannot show the atrophy, and the performance-status report was rewritten from the history (Karnofsky 70, ECOG 1).
Verification pass 7: the history now records the wife's pertinent negatives for mania (no elated or expansive mood, grandiosity, pressured speech or reduced need for sleep, sleep unchanged, no sweating or palpitations), which must 1 and the mania and autoimmune differentials cite.
