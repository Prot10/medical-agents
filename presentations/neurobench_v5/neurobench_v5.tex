  \footnotesize
  Build a benchmark \emph{and} an agent pipeline that measure (and
  improve) \alert{evidence-driven} clinical reasoning. Neurology is
  an ideal test bed (MRI, EEG, EMG, CSF, labs).

\begin{frame}{Medical LLM benchmarks reward trivia, not reasoning}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{How LLMs are evaluated today}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item Datasets: MedQA~\cite{jin2021medqa},
                MedMCQA~\cite{pal2022medmcqa}, USMLE-style sets
          \item Static multiple-choice questions
          \item Every relevant fact is in the stem
          \item Exactly one correct option
          \item Rewards \alert{recall + pattern matching}
        \end{itemize}
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{How clinicians actually work}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item Start with a messy chief complaint
          \item \emph{Decide} which tests to order
          \item Weigh cost, risk, time, stewardship
          \item Resist red herrings and anchoring
          \item Iterate \alert{evidence $\rightarrow$ hypothesis}
        \end{itemize}
      \end{alertblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{7mm}
  \centering\footnotesize
  The exam pre-solves the hardest step of diagnosis:\\[0.5mm]
  {\normalsize\color{CERNblue}\bfseries deciding \emph{what evidence is worth looking at}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The cost of measuring the wrong thing}
  Frontier LLMs reportedly match or beat clinicians on medical
  exams~\cite{singhal2023medpalm2}, yet real deployments tell a
  more complicated story.

  \vspace{3mm}
  What multiple-choice scoring \alert{cannot} measure:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
    \item \textbf{Tool selection:} did the agent order the right test
          at the right time?
    \item \textbf{Tool parameterisation:} with the right panel,
          sequence, or region?
    \item \textbf{Cost and stewardship:} did the agent order three
          MRIs when one would suffice?
    \item \textbf{Red-herring resistance:} did it chase the obvious
          finding past the contradicting one?
    \item \textbf{Reasoning trace:} can a clinician follow the logic
          from chief complaint to plan?
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{3mm}
  MedCaseReasoning~\cite{wu2025medcasereasoning} makes the same point
  on reasoning fidelity; we extend it with \emph{tool use}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two complementary contributions}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{NeuroBench}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item \textbf{365} synthetic from per-condition YAML specs
          \item \textbf{151} seeded from real PMC reports
                (CC-BY 4.0)~\cite{wu2025medcasereasoning,zhao2022pmcpatients}
          \item Evidence moved out of the HPI (history of present
                illness) into \alert{tool outputs}
          \item Rich ground truth: differential, optimal sequence,
                red herrings, contraindicated actions
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{NeuroAgent}
        ReAct~\cite{yao2023react} tool-augmented agent under five
        progressively richer paradigms:
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item Vanilla LLM (no tools): floor
          \item Tool-augmented (12 diagnostic tools)
          \item + SFT on golden trajectories
          \item + GRPO~\cite{shao2024grpo} \emph{(planned)}
          \item + graph-based structured reasoning \emph{(planned)}
        \end{itemize}
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{1mm}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Closest prior work: AgentClinic}
  \scriptsize
  AgentClinic~\cite{schmidgall2024agentclinic} pioneered agentic
  clinical evaluation; NeuroBench targets a complementary axis,
  \alert{evidence-driven tool use}.

  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{cerntable}{@{}p{0.17\textwidth} p{0.37\textwidth} p{0.37\textwidth}@{}}
    \cernheaderrow
    \cernth{Dimension}     & \cernth{AgentClinic (npj Dig.\ Med.\ 2026)}
                           & \cernth{NeuroBench (ours)}                         \\
    Cases                  & 122 (MedQA + NEJM); leakage risk
                           & 516 (synthetic + PMC-seeded); low leakage          \\
    Clinical scope         & General USMLE breadth
                           & Neurology, in depth                                \\
    What it probes         & Doctor--patient dialogue, history-taking
                           & Tool selection on a stripped HPI                   \\
    Tool model             & Measurement agent returns values
                           & 12 typed tools, follow-up branching                \\
    Cost \& stewardship    & \emph{None} (their stated future work)
                           & \alert{Medicare-PFS cost per tool call}            \\
    Scored on              & Diagnosis label only
                           & Dx + optimal / contraindicated actions             \\
    Replay                 & LLM agents: non-deterministic
                           & \alert{Static cache: deterministic, GPU-free}      \\
    Beyond evaluation      & Benchmark only
                           & Benchmark + SFT $\rightarrow$ GRPO training        \\
  \end{cerntable}

  \vspace{0.5mm}
  \centering
  {\color{CERNblue}\bfseries Complementary, not competing:}\;
  dialogue-driven history-taking (AgentClinic) vs.\ evidence-driven
  tool use (ours).
\end{frame}

  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}

  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Two generation pipelines, one schema}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Synthetic pipeline \quad $\color{white}n = 365$}
      Per-condition YAML spec defines disease, expected tool
        outputs, and reasoning rubric.\\[0.5mm]
        LLM receives:
        \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.05em}
          \item Pydantic JSON schema (\texttt{NeuroBenchCase})
          \item Condition YAML
          \item Difficulty tier + encounter type
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Real-PMC-seeded pipeline \quad $\color{white}n = 151$}
        Seed: a published PMC case report
        (\cite{wu2025medcasereasoning}).\\[0.5mm]
        The LLM is instructed to:
        \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.05em}
          \item Preserve the clinical trajectory
          \item \alert{Strip} diagnostic results out of the HPI
          \item Move them into structured tool outputs
          \item Add red herrings at higher difficulties
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{5mm}
  \centering
  Both pipelines emit the same schema. Case IDs starting with
  \texttt{R} are PMC-seeded (e.g.\ \texttt{ALS-RS11}); others are synthetic.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Off-pathway tool calls stay in character}
  \footnotesize
  A case pre-caches outputs only for its diagnostic pathway, yet the
  agent may call any of the 12 tools. A second \alert{fallback tier}
  answers the rest, so the simulation never returns a ``no data
  available'' error.

  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.60\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What an off-pathway tool returns}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.3em}
          \item \textbf{Non-contributory normal} (87\%): a properly
                structured normal report for that modality
          \item \textbf{Incidental finding} (13\%): abnormal but not the
                diagnosis, such as chronic small-vessel change, a benign
                cyst, or trace valvular regurgitation
        \end{itemize}
        Incidental rate calibrated to the imaging-incidentaloma
        literature (15--30\% of scans).
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.36\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Why it matters}
        \footnotesize
        Over-ordering is no longer a free dead-end: it costs money,
        burns turns, and forces the agent to \alert{dismiss red
        herrings it created itself}.
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{1mm}
  \centering\scriptsize\itshape
  2{,}813 fallback outputs, drawn deterministically per (case, tool)
  and flagged for the evaluator, so over-ordering is always scored.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Training: golden trajectories $\rightarrow$ SFT}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{Building the SFT corpus}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item Take the case's ground-truth optimal action sequence
          \item Choreograph a teacher rollout: tool call $\rightarrow$
                cached output $\rightarrow$ short rationale $\rightarrow$
                next call
          \item Verify the trace terminates in the correct diagnosis
                and respects critical / contraindicated actions
          \item Filter by clinician-validated cases only
                ($\kappa$-filtered)
          \item Emit \texttt{(system, user, assistant)} chat tuples
        \end{enumerate}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Targets}
        \footnotesize
        Smaller deployable backbones (7--14\,B). Goal: match larger
        frontier baselines on tool selection and diagnostic accuracy
        at a fraction of the inference cost.
      \end{exampleblock}
      \begin{alertblock}{What SFT does \emph{not} cover}
        \footnotesize
        Teaches imitation of an optimal trajectory, not recovery from
        divergence; that's why we want RL on top.
      \end{alertblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Fine-tuning method: QLoRA on Qwen3.5-9B}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What actually trains}
        \scriptsize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
          \item Base \texttt{Qwen3.5-9B} frozen in \alert{4-bit NF4}
                (BitsAndBytes, double quant, bf16 compute)
          \item LoRA adapters \texttt{r=64}, \texttt{$\alpha$=128} on
                \emph{all} attention + MLP projections
          \item Only adapters update ($\sim$tens of MB), merged for
                deployment; one recipe for SFT \emph{and} RL
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Why QLoRA: a single A100-40\,GB}
        \scriptsize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
          \item Full fine-tuning needs $\sim$4$\times$ the weights for
                optimiser states --- multi-GPU only
          \item 4-bit base shrinks \texttt{18\,GB$\rightarrow$5\,GB},
                leaving room for 6144-token ReAct traces
          \item NF4 + double quant near-lossless: matches 16-bit LoRA
                quality~\cite{dettmers2023qlora}
        \end{itemize}
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \begin{alertblock}{Alternatives considered}
    \scriptsize
    \textbf{Full fine-tuning} (multi-GPU only),
    \textbf{plain 16-bit LoRA} (no memory headroom),
    \textbf{DoRA / LoftQ} (quality upgrade, future ablation), and
    \textbf{prefix / prompt tuning} (too weak for tool use).
  \end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What's next: GRPO and graph-structured reasoning}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{GRPO: relative reasoning RL~\cite{shao2024grpo}}
        \footnotesize
        Sample $k$ trajectories per case, score each, optimise with
        rewards relative to the group mean, with no value network.\\[1mm]
        \textbf{Why it fits:}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.05em}
          \item Reasoning quality is a \alert{relative} signal across
                rollouts on the same case
          \item Our metrics (Dx acc, tool match, cost, red-herring)
                are already group-comparable
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Graph-based structured reasoning}
        \footnotesize
        Replace free-text \texttt{Thought} with a typed
        \emph{clinical reasoning graph}:\\[0.5mm]
        nodes $\in$ \{symptom, exam finding, hypothesis, evidence,
        action, conclusion\}; edges $\in$ \{supports, contradicts,
        triggers, depends-on\}.\\[1mm]
        \textbf{Why:}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.05em}
          \item Constrains hallucination at generation
          \item Makes reasoning \alert{auditable} by clinicians
          \item Maps onto our optimal action sequence
        \end{itemize}
      \end{exampleblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{0.5mm}
  \centering\scriptsize\itshape
  Both are research directions, not yet implemented. The benchmark is
  designed so progress on either is directly visible in the metrics.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Recap and roadmap}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{What we've shown}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
          \item Medical LLM eval needs to move past Q\&A
          \item NeuroBench: 500+ cases, 20 conditions, 12 tools,
                evidence revealed via tool calls
          \item Custom platform for triple-blind clinician validation
                with inter-rater agreement
          \item NeuroAgent: ReAct loop, hospital protocols, memory,
                cost tracking, vLLM on A100s
          \item Variant ladder all measured on the same metrics
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}%
    \hfill
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \begin{exampleblock}{Next steps (paper milestones)}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.05em}
          \item Finish clinician validation on all 516 cases
          \item Lock benchmark + freeze metrics
          \item Full sweep across the variant ladder
          \item Implement GRPO; prototype graph reasoning
          \item Draft for \textbf{Nature Machine Intelligence}
        \end{itemize}
      \end{exampleblock}
      \begin{alertblock}{Open questions}
        \footnotesize
        Metric weighting? Reviewer pool size? Conditions to expand?
      \end{alertblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
