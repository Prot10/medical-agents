### Key Evidence
- Recurrent detailed visual hallucinations for 2 years preceding motor onset, with fluctuating attention/cognition on exam — core DLB clinical features
- REM sleep without atonia (tonic 62%, phasic 48%) on polysomnography confirming RBD, a core synucleinopathy marker present years before parkinsonism
- Severe acute rigidity and obtundation after 0.5 mg haloperidol — characteristic neuroleptic sensitivity reaction
- Reduced bilateral striatal DAT uptake (DaTscan) and occipital hypometabolism with cingulate island sign (FDG-PET) — indicative and supportive DLB biomarkers
- Neuropsych battery showing visuospatial/executive/attention deficits with relatively preserved memory encoding

### Recommendations
1. Start a cholinesterase inhibitor (rivastigmine or donepezil, titrated from low dose) for cognitive fluctuations and hallucinations, with baseline ECG first per dementia protocol
2. If pharmacologic management of hallucinations becomes necessary, use quetiapine, clozapine, or pimavanserin only
3. Review full medication list via hospital drug-interaction check before any new prescription, and continue midodrine for orthostatic hypotension with fall-precaution counseling
4. Counsel family on caregiver support, home safety, and fall-risk precautions given fluctuating cognition and orthostatic drops

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not rechallenge with haloperidol or other typical/most atypical antipsychotics (risperidone, olanzapine, aripiprazole) — documented severe neuroleptic sensitivity reaction
- Do not prescribe metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, or promethazine for nausea — dopamine-blocking agents will worsen parkinsonism
- Avoid centrally acting anticholinergics (trihexyphenidyl, benztropine) — will worsen cognition and hallucinations in this population
- Do not diagnose or treat as Alzheimer's disease without having excluded reversible causes (already done: B12, TSH normal) and considering the DLB-specific pattern above
