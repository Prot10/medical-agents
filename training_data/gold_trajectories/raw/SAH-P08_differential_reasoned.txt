### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from AComA aneurysm with neurogenic Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, complicated by dual antiplatelet therapy (Confidence: 0.72)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Primary acute coronary syndrome - no ST elevation, no reciprocal changes, and echo shows apical ballooning crossing multiple coronary territories rather than a single-vessel distribution
2. Bacterial meningitis - no fever, and CT plus CSF-type findings (non-clearing bloody appearance, xanthochromia) point to hemorrhage rather than infection
3. Aortic dissection - no BP or pulse differential between limbs, and imaging identifies an intracranial aneurysm as the clear source of both the headache and hemodynamic surge

### Key Evidence
- Fisher grade 3 subarachnoid blood on non-contrast CT — confirms SAH as the cause of thunderclap headache and meningismus
- AComA saccular aneurysm 7.8 x 6.4 mm, irregular multilobulated dome on CTA — identifies the bleeding source and guides clipping versus coiling
- Apical ballooning with basal hyperkinesis and EF 30% on echocardiogram — confirms neurogenic Takotsubo rather than coronary-territory infarction despite marked troponin elevation
- Deep T-wave inversions and QTc 532 ms without ST elevation — classic neurogenic ECG pattern of catecholamine surge from SAH

### Recommendations
1. Urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, blood-pressure control (SBP target generally <160 mmHg pre-securing), and cardiac telemetry
4. Hold aspirin and clopidogrel in coordination with cardiology before intervention; consider platelet transfusion if urgent securing is needed
5. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH and avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Initiate daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation — the echocardiogram pattern is neurogenic, and catheterization would delay aneurysm treatment without primary-ACS evidence
- Do not start aspirin or other antiplatelets, or give NSAIDs for headache, before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral or NG only, IV use has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
