- Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures, Fisher grade 3, from CT — confirms SAH and its severity
- 8.4 x 6.2 mm multilobulated AComA aneurysm on CTA — identifies the ruptured source and informs clipping vs coiling
- Communicating hydrocephalus on CT — makes lumbar puncture unsafe and mandates urgent CSF diversion planning
- Diffuse T-wave inversion with QTc 524 ms and troponin/BNP elevation, no ST elevation — neurogenic stress pattern, not primary cardiac ischemia

### Recommendations
1. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and blood pressure control (SBP generally <160 mmHg pre-securing)
2. Start oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours
4. Consult neurosurgery for external ventricular drain given the communicating hydrocephalus
5. Monitor serum sodium closely, avoid hypotonic fluids, and reassess for cerebral salt wasting vs SIADH
6. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT already confirms SAH with hydrocephalus, and CSF removal risks herniation
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs before the aneurysm is secured — increases rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG route only, IV use has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation without independent evidence of primary ACS — this pattern is neurogenic and catheterization would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Avoid uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, but do not over-correct to systemic hypotension
