### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis directly and positively: explain that FND is a real, involuntary condition established by the positive exam findings (Hoover sign, tremor entrainment) and the normal-EEG captured event, not a diagnosis made by "finding nothing."
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for the functional motor symptoms and to CBT/psychological therapy for PNES and comorbid PTSD.
3. Order a comprehensive neuropsychological battery to characterize comorbid depression, anxiety, somatization, and trauma exposure to inform treatment planning.
4. Address alcohol use (daily 2-4 beers, elevated GGT) as part of the integrated care plan given its interaction with sleep, mood, and symptom burden.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue further investigations (EMG/NCS, lumbar puncture, repeat imaging) — with clear positive rule-in signs and unremarkable structural/metabolic workup, additional negative testing reinforces illness conviction and worsens prognosis.
- Do not tell the patient "there is nothing wrong" or "it's all in your head" — this framing damages the therapeutic alliance and is the most common avoidable harm in FND care.
- Do not start antiepileptic drugs for the unresponsive episodes — they are ineffective for PNES and add unnecessary side-effect risk.
- Avoid benzodiazepines as abortive therapy for future witnessed episodes — no benefit for PNES and unnecessary respiratory depression risk.
