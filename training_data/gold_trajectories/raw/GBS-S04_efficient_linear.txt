1. Myasthenic crisis - does not cause conduction slowing, absent F-waves/H-reflexes, or global areflexia
2. Decompensated diabetic peripheral neuropathy - his known DPN is distal and vibration-only; this acute proximal-plus-distal process with areflexia and bulbar involvement is categorically different
3. Acute transverse myelitis / cord compression - no sensory level, no bowel/bladder involvement, and cord lesions do not typically produce diffuse areflexia

- Absent F-waves/H-reflexes, prolonged DML, reduced conduction velocities from EMG/NCS — meets electrodiagnostic criteria for AIDP
- CSF albuminocytologic dissociation (protein 136 mg/dL, WBC 3/mm3) — classic confirmatory finding for GBS
- Influenza A PCR positive — identifies the antecedent immune trigger
- FVC 42% predicted, NIF at threshold (-22 cmH2O) — objective evidence of impending respiratory failure

### Recommendations
1. Intubate and mechanically ventilate; admit to ICU
2. Initiate IVIG (0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days) or plasma exchange as first-line disease-modifying therapy
3. Continuous cardiac telemetry given the autonomic instability characteristic of severe AIDP
4. DVT prophylaxis given immobility
5. Early physical/occupational therapy involvement
6. Repeat EMG in 1-2 weeks to reassess for prognostication and to distinguish AIDP from an axonal variant as the clinical course evolves

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not use corticosteroids as monotherapy — they are not effective in GBS
- Avoid succinylcholine for rapid sequence intubation — risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia in denervated muscle
- Do not delay intubation while awaiting further deterioration — FVC and NIF are already at threshold
- Watch for autonomic swings (bradycardia, labile blood pressure) and hold agents like metoprolol or tamsulosin if instability worsens
