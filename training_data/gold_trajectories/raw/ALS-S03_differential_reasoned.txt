### Primary Diagnosis
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), limb-onset (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Cervical spondylotic myelopathy — MRI shows no compressive cervical lesion, and denervation extends to bulbar and lumbosacral regions well beyond a cervical distribution.
2. Multifocal motor neuropathy — sensory NCS normal but no conduction block was found, which is the defining feature of this mimic.
3. Kennedy disease (bulbospinal muscular atrophy) — bulbar LMN signs are present, but coexisting hyperreflexia, Hoffmans, and Babinski indicate UMN involvement that Kennedy disease does not produce.

### Key Evidence
- Bilateral corticospinal tract T2 hyperintensity with no mass or compressive lesion from MRI — excludes structural mimics while fitting a degenerative UMN process.
- Active denervation and chronic reinnervation in bulbar, cervical, thoracic, and lumbosacral regions with normal sensory NCS and no conduction block from EMG/NCS — meets electrodiagnostic criteria for widespread LMN disease and excludes neuropathic mimics.
- CK 840 U/L (mild-moderate elevation) from labs — consistent with denervation, not a primary myopathy.

### Recommendations
1. Obtain baseline FVC, MIP/MEP, and SNIP to guide timing of noninvasive ventilation initiation.
2. Start riluzole 50 mg twice daily after baseline LFTs, with monthly LFT monitoring for the first 3 months, and check for drug interactions/contraindications before prescribing.
3. Refer to a multidisciplinary ALS clinic for speech/swallowing, nutrition, respiratory, and social-work support.
4. Consider CSF neurofilament light chain testing for prognostic stratification.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not attribute the findings to cervical spondylotic changes or proceed to cervical spine surgery — EMG denervation extends well beyond a single-level cervical distribution.
- Do not defer respiratory assessment — even mild symptomatic reduction in FVC warrants close monitoring and NIV planning.
- Do not initiate steroids, IVIG, or plasma exchange — there is no immunosuppressive indication in ALS and it would only delay correct management.
