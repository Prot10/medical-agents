### Differential Diagnoses
1. Vasovagal syncope — does not explain a persistent third-degree AV block on telemetry over 24 hours; a reflex faint would not produce a fixed conduction abnormality with chronotropic incompetence.

3. Seizure disorder — brief post-ictal confusion and incontinence can occur with any hypoxic arrest; exam is normal, no tongue bite, and the arrhythmia fully accounts for the loss of consciousness.

### Key Evidence
- Third-degree AV block with junctional escape (38 bpm) on initial ECG — the arrhythmic substrate for the arrest.
- Persistent complete AV block with pauses up to 3.8s and chronotropic incompetence on telemetry — confirms a fixed, not transient, conduction disease.
- Lidocaine dose far below LAST toxicity threshold and no AV-nodal blocking agents on her regimen — excludes reversible/iatrogenic causes.

### Recommendations
1. Admit to telemetry for continuous cardiac monitoring pending definitive therapy.
2. Cardiology/EP consult for permanent (dual-chamber) pacemaker implantation — Class I indication for symptomatic third-degree AV block.
3. Document that the periprocedural lidocaine dose is well below the LAST toxicity threshold, so no LAST-specific treatment is indicated.
4. Counsel the patient on driving restriction until pacing is completed and she is cleared.
5. Monitor QTc given escitalopram's contribution; consider sertraline if further prolongation occurs.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not attribute this arrest solely to a vasovagal mechanism — the documented persistent complete AV block is the primary abnormality and must be addressed.
- Do not initiate or continue AV-nodal blocking agents (beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, digoxin), which would worsen the block.
- Do not delay pacemaker planning to await tilt-table testing; tilt-table would only be relevant afterward if a coexisting vasovagal component still needs characterization.
- Do not clear the patient to drive until permanent pacing is completed and cardiology clearance is obtained.
