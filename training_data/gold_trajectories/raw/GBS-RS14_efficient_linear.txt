### Primary Diagnosis
Guillain-Barré syndrome, acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (classic AIDP) (Confidence: 0.92)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Acute transverse myelitis / cervical cord lesion - no sensory level, preserved bulbar/cranial function, and a demyelinating peripheral nerve pattern rather than cord signal change argue against it
2. Myasthenic crisis - global areflexia and stocking-glove sensory loss are not features of a pure neuromuscular junction disorder
3. Toxic or metabolic axonal polyneuropathy - electrodiagnostics show demyelinating (not axonal) features with conduction block and temporal dispersion

### Key Evidence
- Global areflexia with ascending symmetric weakness from history/exam — classic AIDP presentation
- Demyelinating pattern with conduction block, prolonged F-waves, absent H-reflexes from EMG/NCS — confirms acquired demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy
- Albuminocytologic dissociation (protein 88 mg/dL, WBC 2/mm3) from CSF analysis — supports AIDP over infectious causes

### Recommendations
1. Initiate IVIG (0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days) or plasma exchange as first-line disease-modifying therapy
2. Admit for continuous cardiac telemetry given risk of autonomic instability
3. Serial FVC/respiratory monitoring every 4-6 hours given borderline reduced vital capacity

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not use corticosteroids as monotherapy — no proven benefit in AIDP and may delay effective treatment
- Do not defer respiratory monitoring — FVC at 70% predicted can decline rapidly and mandates a low threshold for ICU transfer
