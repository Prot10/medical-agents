### Differential Diagnoses
1. Functional neurological disorder - cannot produce true global areflexia, positive anti-GM1, or an abnormal demyelinating NCS pattern
2. Tick paralysis - also causes ascending areflexic weakness but requires an identified tick and improves with removal; must exclude on skin/scalp exam
3. Other toxic/metabolic distal neuropathy - would not typically cause acute global areflexia with F-wave/H-reflex loss temporally linked to a Campylobacter trigger

### Key Evidence
- Anti-GM1 IgG positive with confirmed Campylobacter jejuni IgM — links antecedent infection to an immune-mediated neuropathy mechanism
- Absent F-waves/H-reflexes, absent sural SNAPs, prolonged DML with preserved CMAP — early demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy pattern on EMG/NCS
- Normal CSF protein at day 3 — does not exclude GBS; albuminocytologic dissociation frequently emerges only after the first week

### Recommendations
1. Admit for serial neurological and respiratory monitoring (FVC/NIF every 4-6 hours) given rapid early progression
2. Initiate IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days now, given the clinical picture meets Brighton level 2-3 criteria despite early/borderline studies
3. Begin continuous cardiac telemetry for autonomic monitoring
4. Repeat EMG/NCS at 7-14 days, as early studies can be falsely reassuring
5. Repeat lumbar puncture in 7-10 days if clinical course continues, since protein elevation often emerges at 1-2 weeks
6. Perform full skin and scalp examination to exclude tick paralysis

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not exclude GBS on the basis of a normal early CSF protein or only mildly abnormal early EMG/NCS — both are well described in the first week of illness
- Do not delay IVIG while awaiting confirmatory repeat CSF or NCS if the clinical picture (ascending paresthesias, global areflexia, antibody and serologic support) is already convincing
- Do not use corticosteroids as monotherapy for GBS
- Monitor closely for respiratory decline (FVC <20 mL/kg, NIF worse than -30 cmH2O) despite currently normal values, given 20-30% of GBS patients require ventilatory support
