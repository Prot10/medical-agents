Early-stage Alzheimer's disease (mild dementia, amnestic) (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Vascular cognitive impairment - white matter changes are only age-appropriate (Fazekas 1), not the dominant imaging finding
2. Depression-related cognitive impairment - two-year progressive functional decline with hippocampal atrophy is inconsistent with a purely mood-driven picture
3. Reversible metabolic dementia (B12/thyroid deficiency) - excluded by normal labs

### Key Evidence
- Bilateral hippocampal atrophy (Scheltens MTA 2-3/3) with temporoparietal-predominant volume loss from MRI — structural hallmark of AD
- Disproportionate, poorly-cued delayed recall loss (1st percentile) from neuropsychological testing — storage-type amnestic signature
- Normal CBC/CMP/TSH/B12/folate — reversible causes excluded

### Recommendations
1. Obtain collateral/informant history detail if not already fully captured, confirming timeline and IADL impact
2. Start a cholinesterase inhibitor (e.g., donepezil) as first-line symptomatic therapy after baseline ECG and drug-interaction check
3. Assess safety: driving fitness, medication and financial management, and caregiver support needs
4. Disclose the diagnosis and prognosis to patient and daughter; discuss advance directives and treatment expectations

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start memantine monotherapy at this mild stage — it is reserved for moderate-to-severe disease
- Do not prescribe anticholinergic medications (e.g., diphenhydramine, oxybutynin) or benzodiazepines, which worsen cognition and fall risk
- Do not initiate cholinesterase inhibitor therapy without a baseline ECG given bradycardia risk, especially with concurrent amlodipine
