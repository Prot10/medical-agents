### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from anterior communicating artery aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.92)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Meningitis - no fever, no altered mentation, and CT shows hemorrhage rather than an inflammatory process
2. Primary/migrainous thunderclap headache - excluded once CT demonstrated frank subarachnoid blood
3. Hypertensive emergency/PRES - blood pressure elevation is present but does not explain the hyperdense cisternal blood or the discrete aneurysm identified on CTA

### Key Evidence
- Thick subarachnoid blood in basal cisterns and Sylvian fissures, Fisher grade 3, from CT — confirms SAH and its severity
- 5.4 x 4.8 mm irregular AComA saccular aneurysm with daughter sac on CTA — identifies the bleeding source and morphology relevant to clipping vs. coiling
- Troponin/BNP elevation with diffuse T-wave inversion and QTc prolongation, no ST elevation, on ECG — consistent with neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy, not primary ACS

### Recommendations
1. Admit to a neurocritical care unit with continuous neurologic checks, cardiac telemetry, and blood-pressure control (target SBP generally <160 mmHg pre-securing)
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours now, continued for 21 days, for delayed cerebral ischemia prophylaxis
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing (coiling or clipping) within 24-72 hours
4. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH and avoid hypotonic fluids
5. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform a lumbar puncture — CT already confirms SAH, and LP adds herniation risk without diagnostic benefit
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs for headache before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not give nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only; IV administration has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for this troponin elevation without strong primary-ACS evidence — it would delay aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
