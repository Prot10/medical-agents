- No coagulopathy on labs — supports a structural (aneurysmal) rather than bleeding-diathesis etiology

- First-degree relative with clipped cerebral aneurysm — supports an aneurysmal, potentially familial, etiology

### Recommendations
1. Obtain CT angiography of the head and neck immediately to characterize the aneurysm(s) and guide clipping versus coiling
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours, continued for 21 days, for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive securing of the ruptured aneurysm within 24-72 hours
4. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neurologic checks, blood pressure control (SBP target generally <160 mmHg pre-securing), and cardiac telemetry
5. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH and avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Initiate daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance
7. Manage the incidental 2.8 mm right ICA aneurysm electively with interval imaging surveillance once the ruptured aneurysm is secured; counsel on first-degree relative screening given the family history

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT has already confirmed SAH, and LP adds no diagnostic value while carrying herniation risk
- Do not start aspirin or other antiplatelet agents before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only; IV administration has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not give NSAIDs for headache control before securing — antiplatelet effect increases rebleeding risk
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the neurogenic troponin elevation without strong primary-ACS evidence — this would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow SBP to exceed 160 mmHg pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
