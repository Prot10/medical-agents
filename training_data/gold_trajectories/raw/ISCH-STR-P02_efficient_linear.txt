- Six-month history of stereotyped, hyperventilation-triggered transient right-hand weakness/word-finding difficulty — classic moyamoya TIA physiology (hypocapnic vasoconstriction unmasking fixed collateral-dependent perfusion).
- No hemorrhage on initial CT and normal glucose/coagulation — clears the acute mimic and thrombolysis-safety labs.

### Recommendations
1. Defer IV alteplase given moyamoya's fragile collateral vessels and individualize any thrombolytic decision with the stroke team and neurosurgery; if thrombolysis is not pursued, start antiplatelet therapy and a high-intensity statin for secondary prevention.
2. Obtain brain MRI with DWI/FLAIR to confirm infarct vintage/territory, transthoracic echocardiogram to exclude a coincidental cardiac source, and continuous telemetry with extended (24h+) cardiac monitoring given that a single normal ECG cannot exclude paroxysmal atrial fibrillation.
3. Discontinue the combined oral contraceptive and counsel on non-hormonal alternatives; refer to neurosurgery for revascularization evaluation (EDAS or STA-MCA bypass).

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not give IV alteplase without individualized hemorrhage-risk discussion — moyamoya's fragile basal collaterals carry an elevated bleeding risk.
- Avoid hypotension, hypovolemia, and hyperventilation/hypocapnia — all can worsen perfusion through collateral-dependent moyamoya territory and precipitate watershed infarction.
- Do not aggressively lower blood pressure in the acute phase outside tPA parameters; maintain permissive pressures to preserve collateral flow.
