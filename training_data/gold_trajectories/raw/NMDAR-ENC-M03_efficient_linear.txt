### Recommendations
1. Send paired CSF and serum anti-NMDAR (GluN1) IgG antibody testing; treat the CSF result as the definitive test rather than relying on serum alone
2. Start empiric IV acyclovir 10 mg/kg every 8 hours until CSF HSV PCR is confirmed negative on final report
3. Initiate first-line immunotherapy now — IV methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 5 days plus IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days (or plasma exchange) — without waiting for antibody confirmation
4. Screen for ovarian teratoma with transvaginal ultrasound and/or contrast-enhanced CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; refer to gynecologic oncology if a lesion is found
5. Start levetiracetam or valproate for seizure control and arrange continuous EEG monitoring to detect non-convulsive status epilepticus
6. Plan for second-line therapy (rituximab or cyclophosphamide) if there is no meaningful clinical improvement by day 10-14

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not use haloperidol or other high-potency D2 antagonists for agitation; they can worsen the movement disorder and mimic neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- Do not delay immunotherapy pending antibody confirmation — turnaround can take 1-2 weeks and delay worsens functional outcome
- Do not anchor on a primary psychiatric diagnosis in a young patient with this constellation without first excluding autoimmune encephalitis
- Do not treat a negative serum antibody result (if it occurs) as ruling out the disease — the CSF assay is the gold standard
