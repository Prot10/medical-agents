### Primary Diagnosis
Functional neurological disorder (conversion disorder) with functional limb weakness and psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Demyelinating disease (e.g., MS) - normal contrast-enhanced brain MRI with no white matter lesions argues strongly against this
2. Structural/compressive lesion - normal brain imaging and intact reflexes/tone make a structural lesion unlikely
3. Epilepsy - video-EEG captured habitual events with no ictal correlate, excluding an epileptic basis for the shaking episodes

### Key Evidence
- Positive Hoover sign and distractible give-way weakness on exam — classic positive rule-in signs for functional limb weakness
- Non-dermatomal sensory loss with midline splitting at the inguinal crease — anatomically inconsistent with an organic nerve or root distribution
- Normal brain MRI with contrast from analyze_brain_mri — excludes structural or demyelinating cause
- Video-EEG capturing four habitual events with no ictal correlate — diagnostic for psychogenic non-epileptic seizures
- Elevated depression/anxiety/PTSD/somatoform dissociation scores and valid neuropsych testing — supports psychiatric comorbidity without indicating cognitive impairment or malingering

### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis transparently using positive language, explaining that the symptoms are real and involuntary and are explained by the positive signs found on exam, with a clear evidence-based treatment path
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for the functional limb weakness and to CBT/psychological therapy for the psychogenic non-epileptic seizures and comorbid mood/anxiety symptoms
3. Taper as-needed clonazepam use where feasible given its lack of a role in treating functional seizures and continue escitalopram for underlying depression/anxiety

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue further negative investigations (e.g., EMG/NCS, lumbar puncture, repeat imaging) once positive rule-in signs and a negative ictal-EEG correlate are established — additional testing reinforces illness conviction and worsens prognosis
- Do not tell the patient nothing is wrong or that the workup found nothing — this damages engagement and is the most common avoidable harm in FND
- Do not start or continue antiepileptic drugs for these events, as they are non-epileptic and AEDs offer no benefit while adding side-effect risk
- Do not administer benzodiazepines as abortive therapy during witnessed events — no benefit and unnecessary sedation/respiratory risk
