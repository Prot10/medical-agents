### Primary Diagnosis
Parkinson's disease (idiopathic), unmasked or exacerbated by concurrent metoclopramide exposure (Confidence: 0.78)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Pure drug-induced parkinsonism — would be expected to show a normal DaTscan and lack premotor non-motor features predating drug exposure; neither holds here
2. Vascular parkinsonism — excluded by normal midbrain/basal ganglia MRI and absence of lower-body predominant gait apraxia
3. Normal pressure hydrocephalus — Evans index at upper-normal limit but proportionate atrophy (ex vacuo) and no classic gait/incontinence/cognitive triad
4. Essential tremor — brother's history noted, but her tremor is postural not resting, and bradykinesia/rigidity/DaTscan findings are not features of ET

### Key Evidence
- Abnormal asymmetric striatal DAT uptake (DaTscan) — distinguishes neurodegenerative PD from pure DIP, which should be normal
- REM sleep without atonia on polysomnography — objective RBD, a synucleinopathy biomarker independent of metoclopramide
- Constipation and hyposmia predating metoclopramide initiation — premotor features pointing to underlying disease rather than drug causation alone
- Normal brain MRI without basal ganglia, vascular, or NPH features — excludes structural mimics
- Metoclopramide flagged as D2 antagonist with black-box warning — mechanistically capable of unmasking/worsening parkinsonism

### Recommendations
1. Discontinue metoclopramide; substitute a non-D2-antagonist prokinetic (domperidone where available, erythromycin, or prucalopride) in coordination with gastroenterology
2. Do not start levodopa while metoclopramide remains in the regimen, given direct pharmacological antagonism
3. Initiate levodopa or another dopaminergic agent only after metoclopramide washout and reassessment of residual parkinsonism
4. Refer to psychiatry/primary care for the GDS-consistent depressive symptoms and continue cognitive monitoring given the MoCA and neuropsychological findings
