### Primary Diagnosis
Behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD), probable, C9orf72-associated (familial) (Confidence: 0.78)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Primary psychiatric disorder (late-onset mania/personality disorder) - does not explain progressive frontal-executive testing deficits or the lobar atrophy pattern on MRI
2. Alzheimer's disease - hippocampal volume and posterior cortex are preserved, and memory impairment is retrieval-pattern rather than the encoding deficit typical of AD
3. Alcohol-related cognitive decline - the behavioral onset (disinhibition, hyperorality) preceded and does not track proportionally with his escalating alcohol use, and the atrophy is lobar rather than diffuse

### Key Evidence
- Bilateral frontal and anterior temporal atrophy with hippocampal/posterior sparing from MRI — matches the expected bvFTD lobar pattern and argues against AD
- FAB 9/18 and Faux Pas 3/20 with retrieval-pattern memory on neuropsych battery — confirms anterior-network executive/social-cognitive deficit meeting Rascovsky 2011 core features (disinhibition, hyperorality, apathy, executive dysfunction) within the required window
- Normal metabolic/infectious labs — excludes reversible mimics
- Paternal early-onset behavioral dementia — raises pretest probability of a familial genetic etiology

### Recommendations
1. Pursue formal genetic testing (C9orf72 first-line, then GRN/MAPT if negative) with pre-test genetic counseling given the familial pattern
2. Screen for ALS features (bulbar symptoms, fasciculations, weakness) at every visit given C9orf72's known ALS overlap
3. Formally document Rascovsky 2011 criteria (≥3 of 6 core features within 3 years) and functional decline in the chart
4. Assess and document decision-making, financial, and driving capacity given his impulsivity and job loss
5. Provide caregiver education, home safety planning, and referral to FTD-focused support resources
6. Offer cascade genetic counseling to first-degree relatives if a pathogenic variant is confirmed

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or memantine — no benefit in bvFTD and may worsen behavioral symptoms
- Avoid antipsychotics as first-line behavioral management given increased cerebrovascular and mortality risk in dementia; reserve for refractory dangerous agitation with specialist oversight
- Avoid benzodiazepines for behavioral control — risk of paradoxical disinhibition and falls
- Do not discharge without registering the patient for continued specialist follow-up and ensuring genetic counseling is arranged before any predictive testing in relatives
