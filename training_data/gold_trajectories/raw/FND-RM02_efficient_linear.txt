### Primary Diagnosis
Functional neurological disorder (conversion disorder) with functional diplopia due to convergence spasm (Confidence: 0.78)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Organic posterior fossa/brainstem lesion — excluded by normal contrast-enhanced brain MRI
2. Myasthenia gravis — no fatigability, ptosis, or fixed ocular restriction; findings inconsistent with a neuromuscular junction pattern
3. Decompensated childhood strabismus — inconsistent, exam-dependent alignment with spontaneous normalization argues against a fixed mechanical/sensory strabismus recurrence

### Key Evidence
- Intermittent convergence-miosis-accommodative triad with spontaneous normalization on repeat exam — the characteristic functional sign complex
- Non-dermatomal midline splitting of vibration sense on forehead testing — a reliable positive rule-in sign for FND
- Normal contrast brain and spine MRI, normal EEG/video-EEG without ictal correlate — excludes structural and epileptic causes
- Elevated PHQ-9 (14) and GAD-7 (16) with valid effort testing (TOMM 50/50) — corroborates comorbid affective burden without confounding cognition

### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis directly and transparently as FND, using the convergence-miosis-accommodative triad and midline sensory split as the positive basis — emphasize the symptoms are real and involuntary, with a clear evidence-based treatment path
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy targeting the functional ocular motor symptom
3. Refer for CBT / psychological therapy to address comorbid depression, anxiety, and psychosocial stressors identified on screening
4. Follow up mildly elevated TSH in primary care; unlikely to be contributing but warrants routine monitoring

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not frame the diagnosis as "nothing wrong" or "all in your head" — this undermines engagement and is the most common avoidable harm in FND
- Avoid further expanding investigations (e.g., repeat imaging, EMG/NCS, lumbar puncture) in the absence of new focal findings — additional negative tests reinforce illness conviction and worsen prognosis
