### Primary Diagnosis
Focal epilepsy of right temporal lobe origin secondary to dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor (DNET) (focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure) (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Ganglioglioma - radiologically similar, but the bubbly multicystic non-enhancing morphology and lack of any nodular enhancing component favor DNET over ganglioglioma.
2. Mesial temporal sclerosis - would not produce a discrete space-occupying T2/FLAIR lesion with mass effect and skull scalloping on MRI.
3. Cavernous malformation - excluded by absence of blooming/hemosiderin signal and by the T2 bubbly cortical architecture, which is not typical of cavernoma.

### Key Evidence
- Olfactory aura ("burning rubber") with rising nausea and oroalimentary automatisms from history — classic mesial temporal seizure semiology.
- Right anterior temporal (F8/T4) spike-wave and TIRDA on EEG — localizes the epileptogenic zone to the right mesial temporal region.
- Non-enhancing, cortically based, bubbly T2/FLAIR lesion in right amygdala/anterior hippocampus on epilepsy-protocol MRI with contrast — characteristic imaging appearance of DNET.

### Recommendations
1. Refer to neurosurgery and neuro-oncology for surgical resection planning; DNETs are typically curative for seizures with gross total resection.
2. Start an AED effective in focal epilepsy (levetiracetam, lamotrigine, or lacosamide) as a bridge to surgery, checking interactions with her current combined oral contraceptive before prescribing per formulary policy.
3. Counsel on driving restriction and seizure precautions, and complete the MRI safety checklist and referral letter to her primary care physician as part of discharge.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not delay neurosurgical referral now that a temporal-lobe tumor has been identified — earlier resection is associated with better seizure freedom.
- Do not prescribe any AED without first checking drug interactions against her combined oral contraceptive and iron supplementation.
- Avoid enzyme-inducing AEDs (e.g., carbamazepine, phenytoin) given interaction potential with hormonal contraception; prefer non-inducing agents.
