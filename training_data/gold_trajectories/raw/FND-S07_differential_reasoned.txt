### Key Evidence
- Positive bilateral Hoover sign on exam — involuntary hip extension with contralateral hip flexion effort, a validated rule-in sign for functional weakness
- Normal brain/spine MRI from analyze_brain_mri — excludes structural or demyelinating cause
- Normal CBC/CMP/TSH/B12 from interpret_labs — excludes reversible metabolic/endocrine mimics
- Preserved, symmetric reflexes with intact sensation on exam — argues against a structural or peripheral neuropathic process

### Recommendations
1. Order a comprehensive neuropsychological battery to characterize comorbid depression, anxiety, somatization, and trauma exposure, informing individualized treatment planning
2. Communicate the diagnosis directly and transparently, using positive language anchored on the Hoover sign — stating the symptoms are real and involuntary, with a clear evidence-based treatment path
3. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for functional motor symptoms and to CBT/psychological therapy for the underlying stress, mood, and anxiety contributors

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue further negative investigations (e.g., EMG/NCS, lumbar puncture) once positive rule-in signs are present and structural/reversible causes are excluded — additional negative testing reinforces illness conviction and worsens prognosis
- Do not frame the diagnosis as "nothing wrong" or "all in your head" — this damages therapeutic alliance and is the most common avoidable harm in FND
- Do not delay engagement with physiotherapy and psychological therapy pending further workup — earlier intervention is associated with better outcomes
