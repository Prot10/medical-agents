### Differential Diagnoses
1. Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage - "worst headache of life" onset is atypical, but the marked neutrophilic CSF pleocytosis, low glucose ratio, and positive Gram stain/PCR are inconsistent with a purely hemorrhagic process.
2. Viral (aseptic) meningitis - would not produce this degree of leukocytosis, procalcitonin elevation, critically low CSF glucose ratio, or Gram-positive organisms on stain.
3. Meningococcal meningitis - clinically similar syndrome, but no petechiae/purpura and both Gram stain and multiplex PCR specifically identify S. pneumoniae, not Neisseria meningitidis.

### Recommendations
1. Draw two sets of peripheral blood cultures before the first antibiotic dose, then immediately administer empiric IV vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg plus ceftriaxone 2 g without delaying beyond 60 minutes of suspicion.
2. Give dexamethasone 10 mg IV before or with the first antibiotic dose, continuing every 6 hours for 4 days.
3. Perform lumbar puncture with the full CSF panel (already obtained) and monitor potassium given the hydrochlorothiazide-dexamethasone interaction.
4. Reassess and de-escalate to ceftriaxone monotherapy once culture and susceptibility confirm a penicillin/cephalosporin-susceptible isolate.
5. Obtain ENT consultation to evaluate the left mastoid as the likely parameningeal source and assess need for surgical drainage if no clinical improvement.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not attribute this presentation to a relapse of the prior ear infection without confirming or excluding meningitis by CSF analysis; the mastoid is a likely portal of entry, not an alternative explanation.
- Do not delay empiric antibiotics while awaiting further imaging or culture results once bacterial meningitis is clinically suspected.
- Do not discontinue dexamethasone early; the 4-day course targets current meningeal inflammation regardless of the prior treated otitis media.
- Maintain droplet precautions until meningococcal disease is definitively excluded.
