### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from right posterior communicating artery aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Bacterial meningitis - no fever, no rash, and CT already shows diffuse subarachnoid blood rather than isolated meningeal enhancement
2. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome - typically recurrent thunderclap headaches over days, not a single event with frank hemorrhage on CT
3. Hypertensive emergency/PRES - headache in PRES is not classically instantaneous-onset and does not produce nuchal rigidity or subarachnoid blood

- Thick hyperdense blood in basal cisterns/Sylvian fissures, Fisher 3, no hydrocephalus — from CT — confirms SAH and its severity grade
- Deep T-wave inversions with QTc prolongation, no ST elevation — from ECG — supports neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy rather than primary ACS, avoiding unnecessary cardiac workup
- Troponin 0.22 and BNP 198 elevated — from labs — consistent with catecholamine-mediated cardiac stress accompanying SAH
- Coital onset with instantaneous peak headache and meningismus — from history/exam — classic thunderclap presentation prompting emergent CT

### Recommendations
1. CT angiography of the head/neck to characterize the aneurysm and guide clipping versus coiling
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg PO every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for aneurysm securing within 24-72 hours
4. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, SBP target <160 mmHg pre-securing, and cardiac telemetry
5. Daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance
6. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH; avoid hypotonic fluids

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT has already confirmed SAH and LP adds no diagnostic value while carrying procedural risk
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs before the aneurysm is secured — increases rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only, IV use has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation absent strong primary-ACS evidence — this is a neurogenic pattern and catheterization would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing; avoid over-correction to systemic hypotension
