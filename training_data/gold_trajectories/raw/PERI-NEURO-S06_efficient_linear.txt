### Primary Diagnosis
Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP) (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Diabetic/metabolic axonal polyneuropathy - excluded by normal HbA1c and by demyelinating (not axonal) NCS pattern
2. Hereditary neuropathy (CMT) - inconsistent with elevated inflammatory markers, conduction block, and CSF albuminocytologic dissociation
3. Paraproteinemic neuropathy - excluded by negative SPEP/monoclonal gammopathy screen

### Key Evidence
- Conduction block, prolonged distal latencies, markedly slowed velocities across multiple nerves from NCS/EMG — meets demyelinating criteria for acquired demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy
- Albuminocytologic dissociation (protein 142 mg/dL, WBC 2 cells/mcL) from CSF analysis — classic supportive finding for CIDP
- Proximal and distal symmetric weakness with diffuse areflexia on exam — pattern consistent with radiculopathic (proximal nerve root) involvement rather than pure distal axonopathy

### Recommendations
1. Check serum IgA and screen for anti-NF155/CNTN1 IgG4 nodal antibodies before treatment — IgA deficiency raises anaphylaxis risk with IVIG, and nodal antibody-positive cases respond preferentially to rituximab rather than IVIG
2. Initiate IVIG 2 g/kg induction as first-line therapy pending safety labs; corticosteroids or plasma exchange are reasonable first-line alternatives depending on comorbidities and access
3. Arrange physical therapy for gait aids and functional support given significant ADL impact
4. Schedule re-evaluation for treatment response and potential de-escalation, as a meaningful proportion of CIDP patients achieve sustained remission

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start IVIG without first confirming serum IgA level — undiagnosed IgA deficiency carries anaphylaxis risk
- Do not stop at symptomatic (pain/pharmacologic) management alone — CIDP is a treatable, immune-mediated process and delaying disease-modifying therapy risks further axonal loss and irreversible disability
