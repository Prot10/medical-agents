### Primary Diagnosis
Behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD), probable, sporadic — advanced stage (Confidence: 0.80)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Atypical/frontal-variant Alzheimer's disease — normal hippocampal volume and only moderate, retrieval-pattern memory impairment argue against it.
2. Alcohol-related cognitive decline — atrophy is lobar frontotemporal with knife-blade gyral pattern, not the diffuse pattern typical of alcohol-related change, and onset preceded the escalation in drinking.
3. Late-onset primary psychiatric disorder — frontal release signs, hyperorality, and severe FAB/executive deficits are not explained by psychiatric illness alone.

### Key Evidence
- Severe bilateral frontal and anterior temporal lobar atrophy with hippocampal/posterior sparing from MRI — classic bvFTD structural signature.
- Normal metabolic/endocrine labs — excludes reversible mimics.
- Severely impaired executive function and social cognition with relatively spared memory on neuropsych testing — anterior-network deficit pattern satisfying Rascovsky 2011 criteria (disinhibition, perseveration, hyperorality, dysexecutive syndrome — well over the 3-of-6 threshold) with clear 2-year functional decline.

### Recommendations
1. Initiate caregiver-mediated dietary control for hyperphagia/weight gain; consider low-dose SSRI (e.g., sertraline) for compulsive and behavioral symptoms.
2. Begin advanced-care planning: discuss long-term care needs, surrogate decision-making, and power of attorney.
3. Formally assess and document decision-making, financial, and driving capacity given prominent impulsivity and impaired judgment.
4. Counsel on driving cessation given markedly elevated crash risk in frontal-network disinhibition syndromes, even with preserved global cognition.
5. Provide caregiver education, home safety planning, and referral to FTD-focused support resources.
6. Offer genetic counselling and discuss cascade testing if a pathogenic variant is identified or suspected.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or memantine — no benefit in bvFTD and may worsen behavioral symptoms.
- Avoid antipsychotics as first-line behavioral therapy given increased cerebrovascular and mortality risk; reserve for refractory dangerous agitation with specialist oversight.
- Avoid benzodiazepines for behavioral control — risk of paradoxical disinhibition and falls.
