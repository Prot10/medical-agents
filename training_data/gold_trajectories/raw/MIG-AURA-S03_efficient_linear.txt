### Primary Diagnosis
Migraine with typical visual aura (ICHD-3 1.2.1) (Confidence: 0.92)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Occipital lobe epilepsy with visual seizures - aura duration (20-25 min) is far longer than the brief (<2 min) stereotyped visual seizures typical of occipital epilepsy

3. Ophthalmologic cause (retinal migraine, ocular pathology) - bilateral, crescent-shaped expanding scotoma with cortical spreading pattern is characteristic of cortical (not retinal) aura, and vision is normal between episodes

### Key Evidence
- Expanding fortification spectra over 20-25 min followed by unilateral throbbing headache with photophobia/nausea from history — classic cortical spreading depression pattern of typical migraine aura
- ICHD-3 1.2 criteria confirmed and no SNOOP red flag identified from literature search — supports a primary headache diagnosis without need for neuroimaging

### Recommendations
1. Initiate sumatriptan (or rizatriptan) at aura resolution/headache onset as first-line acute therapy, replacing as-needed ibuprofen
2. Counsel on gradual caffeine taper (from 3-4 cups/day toward ~1 cup/day) and regular sleep schedule to address likely triggers behind the recent escalation
3. Given the increasing frequency in the setting of PhD-related stress, discuss initiating preventive therapy (e.g., topiramate or propranolol) if attacks reach or exceed 4/month going forward

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start combined oestrogen-progestogen contraception in a migraine-with-aura patient given the associated ischemic stroke risk
- Do not co-administer ergotamines with triptans due to risk of prolonged vasoconstriction
- Monitor for medication overuse: ibuprofen use should be limited to <10-15 days/month to avoid analgesic-overuse headache
