### Primary Diagnosis
Focal epilepsy of right mesial temporal lobe origin secondary to dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor (DNET) (Confidence: 0.92)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Mesial temporal sclerosis - MRI shows a discrete non-enhancing mass with bubbly architecture rather than atrophic, hyperintense hippocampal sclerosis.
2. Ganglioglioma - possible on imaging alone, but the well-circumscribed multicystic "bubbly" morphology with bright ring sign is more characteristic of DNET; histology at resection will confirm.
3. Panic/anxiety spells - stereotyped olfactory-epigastric aura, automatisms, and a concordant right anterior temporal epileptiform focus on EEG make an anxiety etiology implausible.

### Key Evidence
- Right anterior temporal spike-and-slow-wave complexes and TIRDA from EEG — confirms a focal epileptogenic source localizing to the mesial temporal region.
- Non-enhancing bubbly right amygdala/hippocampal lesion from MRI epilepsy protocol — identifies the structural substrate co-localizing with the EEG focus and aura semiology.
- Olfactory hallucination with rising epigastric sensation preceding impaired awareness — classic uncinate/mesial temporal aura pattern.

### Recommendations
1. Initiate a first-line focal-epilepsy AED (levetiracetam, lamotrigine, or lacosamide) while surgical evaluation proceeds.
2. Refer to a comprehensive epilepsy center / neurosurgery for presurgical evaluation and resection planning, given the high likelihood of seizure freedom with lesionectomy.
3. Counsel the patient on driving restrictions and seizure-safety precautions per state regulations.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not refer for an ENT workup for the olfactory hallucinations — this is a seizure aura, not a rhinologic complaint.
- Do not perform a lumbar puncture; there are no signs of CNS infection or inflammation to justify one.
- Avoid starting carbamazepine without first checking baseline sodium and, if applicable, HLA-B*1502 status, given the enzyme-induction and hyponatremia risks.
