### Key Evidence
- Intimal flap, crescent mural hematoma, and string sign on CTA/MRA of the left ICA — direct structural confirmation of dissection
- DWI/ADC negative on MRI — no completed infarct, still within the preventable window
- NIHSS 0, normal ECG/Holter, normal echocardiogram — excludes competing cardioembolic mechanism and confirms he has not evolved a fixed deficit

### Recommendations
1. Begin antiplatelet therapy (aspirin, transitioning to low-dose maintenance) per CADISS-guided shared decision-making; discontinue ibuprofen given antiplatelet interaction and discontinue sumatriptan given vasoconstrictive risk in a dissected vessel
2. Initiate continuous telemetry during admission and arrange outpatient extended cardiac monitoring given cryptogenic-stroke-equivalent presentation
3. Address atherogenic lipid profile with high-intensity statin and structured smoking cessation counseling
4. Serial vascular imaging follow-up (MRA/CTA) at 3-6 months to document dissection healing

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not attribute new unilateral retro-orbital pain with Horner syndrome to migraine history — vessel imaging is mandatory regardless of prior headache diagnosis
- Do not restart sumatriptan or other triptans while the dissection is active — vasoconstriction risks worsening luminal narrowing
- Do not delay antithrombotic initiation now that hemorrhage and coagulopathy have been excluded — the pre-infarct window is the opportunity to prevent stroke, not just treat it
