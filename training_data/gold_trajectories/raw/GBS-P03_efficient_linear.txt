3. Transverse myelitis / structural polyradiculopathy - normal cranial nerves, no bowel/bladder involvement, and a peripheral (not cord-level) demyelinating pattern on NCS argue against a cord lesion.

- Positive anti-GM1 IgG and Campylobacter IgM from interpret_labs — links the antecedent infection to a post-infectious immune-mediated neuropathy.

### Recommendations
1. Admit for serial respiratory (FVC/NIF q4-6h) and neurological monitoring given rapid early progression.
2. Initiate IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days now, without waiting for repeat CSF or EMG confirmation.
3. Begin continuous cardiac telemetry for autonomic instability surveillance.
4. Repeat EMG/NCS at 7-14 days since this initial study, while abnormal, may still understate the extent of demyelination.
5. Repeat lumbar puncture in 7-10 days, as albuminocytologic dissociation often emerges only at 1-2 weeks.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not dismiss the diagnosis because CSF protein and early EMG are only mildly abnormal — both are well-described in the first week of GBS.
- Do not delay IVIG pending confirmatory repeat testing if the clinical picture (ascending symptoms, global areflexia, antecedent trigger) remains convincing.
- Do not use corticosteroids as monotherapy — they are not effective as sole treatment for GBS.
