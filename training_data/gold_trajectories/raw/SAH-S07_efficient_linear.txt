### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from right paraclinoid ICA aneurysm in PKD patient (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.78)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Bacterial meningitis - fever and gradual onset over hours typically expected; here onset was instantaneous with CT-proven blood, not infection
2. Primary (benign) thunderclap headache - cannot be diagnosed until SAH is excluded by imaging; CT here is positive
3. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome - usually recurrent thunderclap episodes without this degree of CT-confirmed hemorrhage

### Key Evidence
- Hyperdense subarachnoid blood in basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures, Fisher 3 — from CT head, confirms SAH and predicts high vasospasm risk
- Neurogenic T-wave inversions and mild troponin elevation without ST changes — from ECG/labs, consistent with stress cardiomyopathy rather than primary cardiac event, avoiding unnecessary catheterization
- PKD with strong family history of ruptured/unruptured aneurysm — from history, raises pretest probability of an underlying aneurysm as the bleed source

### Recommendations
1. Obtain CT angiography of the head and neck with contrast-allergy premedication to localize and characterize the aneurysm.
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis; hold nifedipine during this course given additive dihydropyridine calcium-channel-blocker effect.
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours.
4. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and blood pressure control (SBP target <160 mmHg pre-securing).
5. Monitor serum sodium closely and avoid hypotonic fluids given risk of cerebral salt wasting/SIADH.
6. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT has already confirmed SAH, and LP adds no diagnostic value while carrying procedural and herniation risk.
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs for headache before the aneurysm is secured — antiplatelet effect increases rebleeding risk, which carries roughly 50% mortality.
- Do not give nimodipine intravenously — oral or nasogastric administration only, as IV use has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse.
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation absent strong primary-ACS evidence — this reflects neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy and catheterization would delay definitive aneurysm treatment.
