Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from left MCA bifurcation aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.92)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Bacterial meningitis - meningismus present, but no fever and CT shows hyperdense cisternal blood rather than infectious changes
2. Complicated migraine - headache reached peak intensity in seconds, far faster than a migraine's typical crescendo, and CT is positive for blood
3. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome - can cause thunderclap headache, but does not explain hyperdense subarachnoid blood on CT

### Key Evidence
- Instantaneous peak-in-seconds thunderclap headache with vomiting and meningismus from Presentation — classic SAH history
- Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures, Fisher 3, no hydrocephalus/IVH from CT head — confirms SAH and stratifies vasospasm risk
- 5.0 x 4.4 mm irregular saccular aneurysm at left MCA bifurcation from CTA — identifies the bleed source and guides securing strategy
- Amlodipine-nimodipine additive CCB interaction from check_drug_interactions — requires holding amlodipine during nimodipine course

### Recommendations
1. Urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours; conventional DSA for treatment planning
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis, holding amlodipine for the duration and monitoring BP before each dose
3. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and BP control (target SBP <160 mmHg pre-securing)
4. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH; avoid hypotonic fluids
5. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance
6. Discontinue the oral contraceptive given the increased hemorrhagic/thrombotic risk after SAH; discuss non-hormonal alternatives once stabilized

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT already confirms SAH and LP adds no diagnostic value while carrying herniation risk
- Do not start aspirin, NSAIDs, or other antiplatelet agents before the aneurysm is secured — increases rebleeding risk, which carries roughly 50% mortality
- Do not give nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only; IV administration has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
- Do not chase the mildly elevated troponin with cardiac catheterization — the pattern is consistent with neurogenic stress response, not primary ACS, and pursuing it would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
