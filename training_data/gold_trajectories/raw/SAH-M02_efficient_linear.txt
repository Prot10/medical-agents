### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from anterior communicating artery aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.72)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Non-aneurysmal perimesencephalic SAH - CTA identifies a discrete AComA aneurysm rather than isolated perimesencephalic blood pattern
2. Bacterial/viral meningitis - CT shows frank hemorrhage rather than an infectious process, and there is no fever
3. Vascular malformation (AVM) - CTA shows a discrete saccular aneurysm, not a nidus, at the culprit site

### Key Evidence
- Non-contrast CT with CTA: hyperdense blood in basal cisterns/Sylvian fissures with IVH and hydrocephalus (Fisher 3), plus a 5.2 x 4.8 mm bilobed AComA aneurysm — confirms SAH, its severity, and localizes the bleeding source to drive clipping/coiling planning
- ECG and troponin/BNP: neurogenic T-wave inversions and QTc prolongation with modest troponin rise — reflects stress cardiomyopathy rather than primary cardiac disease, avoiding an unnecessary cath lab detour

### Recommendations
1. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and BP control (SBP target generally <160 mmHg pre-securing)
2. Start oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis, holding amlodipine during the course and monitoring BP before each dose
3. Urgent joint neurosurgery/interventional neuroradiology consultation for aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours, with DSA for definitive characterization
4. Emergent neurosurgery consult for external ventricular drain given the acute hydrocephalus
5. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH; avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT already confirms SAH with IVH and hydrocephalus, so LP adds no diagnostic value and risks herniation
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs before the aneurysm is secured — increases rebleeding risk
- Do not give nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only; IV administration has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation — pattern is consistent with neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy, and workup would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
