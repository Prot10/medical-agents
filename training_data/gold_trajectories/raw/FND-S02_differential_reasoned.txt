### Primary Diagnosis
Functional neurological disorder (conversion disorder) with motor symptoms (right leg weakness) and psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Structural CNS lesion (demyelinating disease, mass) - normal brain MRI with contrast excludes a corresponding structural abnormality
2. Focal epilepsy with atypical semiology - captured habitual event showed no ictal EEG correlate on video-EEG
3. Electrolyte/endocrine-mediated weakness or seizure - sodium and thyroid values not low enough to account for focal weakness or convulsive episodes

### Key Evidence
- Positive Hoover sign and give-way weakness pattern from bedside exam — a reliable positive rule-in sign for functional weakness
- Non-dermatomal sensory loss with sharp midline split — inconsistent with a single structural neuroanatomic lesion
- Normal brain MRI with contrast — excludes structural/demyelinating cause
- Captured habitual shaking event with no electrographic correlate on video-EEG — diagnostic for PNES

### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis directly and transparently, framing FND as a real and treatable condition, anchored on the positive rule-in signs observed on exam
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for the functional motor symptoms
3. Refer for CBT/psychological therapy targeting PNES and comorbid anxiety, depression, and trauma exposure
4. Order a comprehensive neuropsychological battery to characterize comorbid mood, anxiety, and trauma-related symptoms and inform treatment planning
5. Review current medications for agents that could exacerbate symptoms as part of ongoing management

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue further negative structural or electrophysiologic workup once positive rule-in signs, normal MRI, and a normal ictal EEG are established — additional testing reinforces illness conviction and worsens prognosis
- Do not frame the diagnosis as "nothing is wrong" or "it's all in your head" — this undermines the therapeutic alliance and engagement with treatment
- Avoid initiating antiepileptic drugs for these episodes — they are ineffective for PNES and add unnecessary side-effect burden
- Do not give benzodiazepines during a witnessed episode — no benefit for PNES and risk of respiratory depression
