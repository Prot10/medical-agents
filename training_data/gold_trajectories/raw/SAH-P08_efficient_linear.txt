2. Aortic dissection - no pulse/BP differential or mediastinal widening reported; headache/meningismus and CT findings are better explained by SAH
3. Hypertensive emergency headache - severity, thunderclap onset, and meningismus with CT-confirmed blood argue for a structural bleed, not hypertensive headache alone

### Key Evidence
- Fisher grade 3 subarachnoid blood on non-contrast CT — confirms the bleed and its location
- 7.8 x 6.4 mm irregular AComA aneurysm on CTA — identifies the culprit lesion for securing decisions
- Diffuse T-wave inversion with prolonged QTc and no ST elevation/reciprocal changes on ECG — supports neurogenic cardiac injury over ACS
- Troponin 2.8 ng/mL and BNP 486 with the above ECG pattern — consistent with stress (Takotsubo) cardiomyopathy rather than coronary occlusion

### Recommendations
1. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and SBP control generally <160 mmHg pre-securing
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg PO every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Urgent neurosurgery/interventional neuroradiology consultation for clipping vs. coiling within 24-72 hours
4. Hold aspirin and clopidogrel with cardiology input given the coronary stent, and pursue coordinated antiplatelet management before intervention
5. Monitor serum sodium for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH and avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Initiate daily transcranial Doppler days 4-14 for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT already confirmed SAH, and LP adds no diagnostic value while risking herniation-related complications
- Do not start or continue antiplatelet agents (including additional aspirin) before the aneurysm is secured — rebleeding risk is high and carries ~50% mortality
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization based on troponin/ECG alone without primary ACS evidence — this would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG route only, given risk of fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not give NSAIDs for headache control before securing — antiplatelet effect increases rebleeding risk
