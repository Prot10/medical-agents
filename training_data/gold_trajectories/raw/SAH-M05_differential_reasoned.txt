- Mild hyponatremia (134) with communicating hydrocephalus on CT — raises early concern for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH requiring close sodium monitoring

### Recommendations
1. Urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing (clipping or coiling) within 24-72 hours
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, BP control (SBP target generally <160 mmHg pre-securing), and cardiac telemetry
4. Consult neurosurgery regarding external ventricular drain given acute hydrocephalus
5. Monitor serum sodium closely and avoid hypotonic fluids given cerebral salt wasting/SIADH risk
6. Initiate daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT already confirms SAH with hydrocephalus, so LP carries herniation risk and adds no diagnostic value
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG only, IV use has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin/echo findings — wall motion pattern and ECG support neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy, and catheterization would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Avoid uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, but do not over-correct into systemic hypotension
