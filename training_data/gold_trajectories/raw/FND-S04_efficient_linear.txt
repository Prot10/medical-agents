Functional neurological disorder (conversion disorder) with weakness and psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Multiple sclerosis / demyelinating disease - normal contrast-enhanced brain MRI with no T2/FLAIR lesions
2. Focal epilepsy - five habitual events captured on video-EEG with no ictal correlate and normal interictal background
3. Structural spinal cord lesion - non-dermatomal sensory loss with midline split is inconsistent with a structural cord level

### Key Evidence
- Positive Hoover sign and give-way weakness normalizing with distraction — bedside rule-in signs establishing functional weakness
- Non-dermatomal sensory loss with sharp midline split — incompatible with a peripheral or central anatomical lesion
- Normal brain MRI with contrast — excludes structural/demyelinating cause
- Video-EEG capturing five habitual shaking events with no ictal correlate and normal interictal EEG — diagnostic for psychogenic non-epileptic events
- Elevated PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5 with passed symptom validity testing — supports genuine psychiatric comorbidity, not malingering

### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis directly and empathetically, framing it around the positive rule-in signs (Hoover sign, tremor entrainment) and emphasizing the symptoms are real, involuntary, and treatable
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for the functional leg weakness and gait pattern
3. Refer for CBT/psychological therapy targeting the psychogenic non-epileptic events and comorbid PTSD, depression, and anxiety

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not pursue further negative investigations (e.g., EMG/NCS, lumbar puncture) — repeated negative workups reinforce illness conviction and worsen prognosis
- Do not tell the patient "nothing is wrong" or "it's all in your head" — undermines the therapeutic alliance
- Do not start antiepileptic drugs for the non-epileptic events and do not give benzodiazepines as abortive therapy during witnessed episodes
