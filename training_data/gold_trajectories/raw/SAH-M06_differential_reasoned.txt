### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from anterior communicating artery aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.82)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Primary acute coronary syndrome - troponin rise and T-wave inversions lack ST elevation or Q waves, consistent with neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy rather than infarction
2. Bacterial meningitis - CT shows diffuse subarachnoid blood rather than isolated inflammatory/purulent process, and headache onset was instantaneous rather than subacute
3. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome - explosive headache pattern overlaps, but CT already demonstrates frank subarachnoid blood rather than a normal or vasoconstrictive-only picture

### Key Evidence
- Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures, Fisher 3 from CT — confirms subarachnoid hemorrhage as the primary process
- 5.8 x 4.6 mm lobulated AComA aneurysm on CTA — identifies the bleeding source and informs clipping vs coiling planning
- Troponin 0.11 with diffuse T-wave inversions and QTc prolongation, no ST elevation — consistent with neurogenic stress response, not primary cardiac disease

### Recommendations
1. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and BP control (SBP target <160 mmHg pre-securing)
2. Start oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours for 21 days for vasospasm prophylaxis (oral/NG only, never IV)
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for aneurysm clipping vs coiling within 24-72 hours
4. Check drug interactions before any medication changes, including reassessment of oral contraceptive use given thrombotic risk post-SAH
5. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH; avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Begin daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT is already diagnostic for SAH and LP adds herniation risk without benefit
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs for headache before the aneurysm is secured — increases rebleeding risk
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation without strong independent evidence of primary ACS — would delay definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow SBP to exceed 160 mmHg pre-securing, and avoid over-correction to systemic hypotension
