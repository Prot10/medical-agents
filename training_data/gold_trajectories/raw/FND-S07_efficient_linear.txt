### Primary Diagnosis
Functional neurological disorder — bilateral functional leg weakness (paraplegia) (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Cervical/thoracic myelopathy or myelitis — excluded by normal brain and spine MRI with no cord signal abnormality or compression
2. Guillain-Barré / acute inflammatory polyneuropathy — argues against: normal, symmetric 2+ reflexes and no Babinski, inconsistent with an acute demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy
3. Metabolic or endocrine myopathy (thyroid, B12) — excluded by entirely normal lab panel

### Key Evidence
- Positive bilateral Hoover sign on exam — involuntary normal-strength hip extension during contralateral flexion, a reliable rule-in sign for functional weakness
- Normal brain and spine MRI (`analyze_brain_mri`) — excludes structural, compressive, and demyelinating causes
- Normal labs (`interpret_labs`) — excludes reversible metabolic/endocrine mimics
- Literature review (`search_medical_literature`) — supports FND-specific physiotherapy and CBT as evidence-based first-line treatment

### Recommendations
1. Communicate the diagnosis directly and transparently: state clearly this is functional neurological disorder, the weakness is real and involuntary, and explain the diagnosis using the positive Hoover sign as concrete evidence rather than as an absence of findings.
2. Refer to FND-informed physiotherapy for functional motor retraining and to CBT/psychological therapy to address the precipitating academic stress, sleep disruption, and mood symptoms.
3. Obtain a comprehensive neuropsychological battery to characterize comorbid depression, anxiety, somatization, and trauma exposure and inform the treatment plan.
4. Review current medications for symptom-exacerbating agents before any further prescribing.

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not tell the patient "nothing is wrong" or "it's all in your head" — this framing damages engagement and is the most common avoidable harm in FND care.
- Do not pursue further negative structural or electrodiagnostic workup (e.g., EMG/NCS, repeat imaging) now that a positive rule-in sign and reassuring imaging/labs are in hand — additional negative testing reinforces illness conviction and worsens prognosis.
- Do not delay physiotherapy/CBT referral pending psychiatric evaluation — psychiatric comorbidity informs but does not gate initiation of FND-specific treatment.
