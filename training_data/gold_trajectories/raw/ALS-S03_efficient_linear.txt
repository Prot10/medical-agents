### Primary Diagnosis
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), limb-onset (Confidence: 0.90)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Cervical spondylotic myelopathy — bulbar denervation and lumbosacral/thoracic involvement cannot be explained by a cervical lesion, and MRI shows no compressive pathology
2. Multifocal motor neuropathy — normal sensory NCS is expected, but absence of conduction block and presence of bulbar/upper motor neuron signs argue against it
3. Kennedy disease (spinobulbar muscular atrophy) — bulbar and limb LMN findings overlap, but prominent upper motor neuron signs (Hoffmann, Babinski, spasticity, brisk jaw jerk) are not typical

- Bilateral corticospinal tract signal change with no mass or compressive lesion from Brain MRI — excludes structural mimics
- Active/chronic denervation across bulbar, cervical, thoracic, and lumbosacral regions with normal sensory NCS and no conduction block from EMG/NCS — confirms widespread lower motor neuron disease and excludes treatable neuropathies
- CK 840 U/L (2-5x ULN) from labs — consistent with denervation, not a primary myopathy
- Combined UMN (hyperreflexia, Hoffmann, Babinski, spastic gait, brisk jaw jerk) and LMN (tongue and thenar atrophy, fasciculations) signs on exam — satisfies the core diagnostic requirement for motor neuron disease

### Recommendations
1. Obtain baseline pulmonary function (FVC, MIP/MEP, SNIP) to guide timing of non-invasive ventilation
2. Check drug interactions and baseline LFTs, then initiate riluzole 50 mg twice daily with monthly LFT monitoring for the first 3 months
3. Refer to a multidisciplinary ALS clinic for speech-language, dietetic, respiratory, and social-work input
4. Consider CSF neurofilament light chain for prognostic stratification

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not proceed to cervical spine surgery on the basis of incidental spondylotic change — EMG denervation extends well beyond the cervical cord
- Do not start steroids, IVIG, or plasma exchange — no benefit in ALS and risks delaying correct management
- Do not defer respiratory assessment even though the patient is not currently in distress — early monitoring determines NIV timing
- Do not attribute the presentation to a functional or psychiatric cause given the objective EMG and exam findings
