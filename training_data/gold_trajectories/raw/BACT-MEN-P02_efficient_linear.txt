1. Partially-treated pyogenic bacterial meningitis - only mildly elevated procalcitonin and a subacute granulomatous imaging/lab pattern are atypical for pyogenic infection

### Recommendations
1. Initiate empiric four-drug anti-tuberculous therapy (RIPE) within hours of this culture/imaging correlation
2. Start adjunctive dexamethasone 0.4 mg/kg/day IV with a 6-8 week taper, together with (never instead of) anti-tuberculous therapy
3. Send CSF AFB stain, MTB PCR (Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra), mycobacterial culture, and ADA from the drain-sourced sample prior to any further steroid dose adjustment, since steroids can alter cellularity
4. Continue close neurosurgical involvement for the communicating hydrocephalus given elevated opening pressure - CSF diversion via the drain may need to continue
5. Initiate HIV testing and complete the systemic TB workup (sputum studies) building on the chest CT findings
6. Report the case to public health authorities and initiate close-contact tracing

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform a diagnostic lumbar puncture without neurosurgical clearance given the hydrocephalus and elevated opening pressure - risk of cerebral herniation
- Do not withhold empiric RIPE therapy while awaiting further mycobacterial susceptibility results, which can take weeks
- Do not rely on the initially negative Gram stain/bacterial culture to fully exclude bacterial co-infection or to stop broad-spectrum coverage prematurely without clinical correlation
- Do not start corticosteroids without concurrent anti-tuberculous therapy
