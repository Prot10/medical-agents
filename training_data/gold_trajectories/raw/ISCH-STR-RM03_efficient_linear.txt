### Recommendations
1. Establish exact time of last known well and apply NIHSS (currently 0) to formally document thrombolysis eligibility, understanding no completed deficit is present
2. Admit to a monitored stroke unit with continuous telemetry and arrange 24-hour Holter (extending to event monitoring if source remains unclear) to detect paroxysmal atrial fibrillation
3. Maintain blood pressure below 185/110 mmHg if thrombolytic therapy is considered, and avoid aggressive lowering below 140/90 mmHg otherwise to preserve perfusion across the narrowed segment
4. Initiate antithrombotic therapy (antiplatelet preferred per CADISS trial data, e.g., aspirin, given similar efficacy to anticoagulation with lower bleeding risk) for secondary stroke prevention
5. Start a high-intensity statin given the elevated LDL and overall vascular risk profile
6. Discontinue sumatriptan and ibuprofen — replace triptan with a non-vasoconstrictive migraine alternative and switch analgesia to acetaminophen
7. Counsel on smoking cessation as a modifiable dissection risk factor

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not administer IV tPA before non-contrast CT has excluded hemorrhage — already done here, but this sequence must not be skipped in any stroke-alert patient
- Do not anchor on cluster headache or migraine given the new painful Horner syndrome — vessel imaging is mandatory before dismissing the pain as benign
- Do not continue sumatriptan — triptans are vasoconstrictive and contraindicated in the setting of an actively dissecting vessel
- Do not delay antithrombotic initiation once hemorrhage and coagulopathy are excluded — early treatment reduces the risk of progression to completed stroke
