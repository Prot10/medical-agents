### Primary Diagnosis
Organophosphate poisoning with cholinergic toxidrome and nicotinic CNS seizures presenting as apparent non-convulsive status epilepticus, complicated by intermediate syndrome at 48 hours (Confidence: 0.70)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Septic shock with secondary seizures - fever/tachycardia/hypotension fit, but critically suppressed plasma and RBC cholinesterase and normal CT are not explained by infection
2. Primary generalized convulsive status epilepticus (idiopathic) - EEG shows a diffuse beta coma pattern rather than a classic ictal buildup, and cholinesterase suppression better explains both the movements and coma
3. Sympathomimetic/stimulant overdose - would produce mydriasis and tachycardia but not profuse hypersalivation, bronchorrhea, or cholinesterase suppression

### Key Evidence
- Plasma cholinesterase 0.18 U/mL and RBC cholinesterase 0.8 U/mL from interpret_labs — critically suppressed, specific confirmation of organophosphate exposure that a routine tox screen would not detect
- Diffuse beta coma pattern with possible ictal theta bursts from analyze_eeg — consistent with cholinergic CNS excess rather than focal structural or infectious pathology
- Normal head CT — excludes structural cause, supports toxic-metabolic etiology
- Mydriasis, hypersalivation, hyperthermia, tachycardia, rhabdomyolysis (CK 8640) — cholinergic/nicotinic toxidrome with muscle hyperactivity, not sepsis

### Recommendations
1. Immediate decontamination — remove clothing, wash skin
2. Atropine IV titrated to bronchial secretion control (not heart rate), large doses often required
3. Pralidoxime 1-2 g IV as early as possible, before enzyme aging occurs
4. Benzodiazepines (lorazepam/diazepam) as first-line for seizure activity
5. Secure airway with mechanical ventilation using rocuronium (not succinylcholine) if intubation required
6. Correct hypoglycemia and hypokalemia; monitor respiratory function (NIF/FVC) serially for emerging intermediate syndrome
7. Toxicology/poison control consultation and ICU admission

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not use succinylcholine for intubation — pseudocholinesterase inhibition causes markedly prolonged paralysis
- Do not use phenytoin as first-line seizure treatment — ineffective against the acetylcholine-excess mechanism
- Avoid morphine — worsens bronchorrhea and respiratory depression in cholinergic crisis
- Do not extubate prematurely — anticipate intermediate syndrome at 24-96h with delayed respiratory muscle failure requiring continued ventilation
