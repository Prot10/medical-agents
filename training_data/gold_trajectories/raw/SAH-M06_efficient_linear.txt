### Primary Diagnosis
Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage from anterior communicating artery aneurysm (Hunt-Hess 2, Fisher 3) (Confidence: 0.80)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Meningitis/encephalitis - no fever, and CT already shows frank subarachnoid blood explaining the meningismus without need for CSF sampling
2. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome - typically lacks a discrete hyperdense cisternal blood pattern and an angiographically visible saccular aneurysm
3. Primary cardiac ischemia (ACS) - ECG changes and troponin rise lack ST-elevation and fit a neurogenic stress pattern temporally linked to the hemorrhage

### Key Evidence
- Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns and bilateral Sylvian fissures, Fisher grade 3 from CT — confirms SAH and quantifies vasospasm risk
- 5.8 x 4.6 mm lobulated saccular aneurysm at the AComA from CTA — identifies the bleeding source and informs clipping vs coiling
- Diffuse T-wave inversion and QTc prolongation without ST-elevation from ECG — consistent with neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy, not ACS
- Mild leukocytosis and troponin elevation from labs — stress response supporting the SAH diagnosis rather than an alternate infectious or cardiac process

### Recommendations
1. Admit to neurocritical care with continuous neuro checks, cardiac telemetry, and blood-pressure control targeting SBP <160 mmHg pre-securing
2. Start nimodipine 60 mg orally every 4 hours now, continued for 21 days, for vasospasm/delayed cerebral ischemia prophylaxis
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for clipping vs coiling within 24-72 hours
4. Trend serum sodium closely and avoid hypotonic fluids given risk of cerebral salt wasting/SIADH
5. Begin daily transcranial Doppler surveillance days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT has already confirmed SAH, and LP adds bleed and herniation risk without diagnostic benefit here
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelet agents, or NSAIDs for headache before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not give nimodipine intravenously — oral/NG route only, IV administration has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation absent strong primary-ACS evidence — delays definitive aneurysm treatment
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, but avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
