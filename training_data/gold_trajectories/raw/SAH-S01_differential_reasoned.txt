### Key Evidence
- Thick hyperdense blood in basal cisterns, bilateral Sylvian fissures, and interhemispheric fissure from CT — confirms SAH and gives Fisher grade 3
- Diffuse T-wave inversion with QTc prolongation, no ST elevation, from ECG — pattern of neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy rather than ACS
- Troponin and BNP elevation from labs — supports catecholamine-mediated cardiac stress rather than a competing cardiac diagnosis needing catheterization
- Guideline evidence for nimodipine (Class I, Level A) and 24-48h securing window from literature search — anchors the treatment plan

### Recommendations
1. Obtain CT angiography of the head and neck now to characterize the aneurysm and guide clipping versus coiling
2. Start oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours, continued for 21 days, for vasospasm prophylaxis
3. Obtain urgent neurosurgery and interventional neuroradiology consultation for definitive aneurysm securing within 24-72 hours
4. Admit to a neurocritical care unit with continuous neuro checks, blood pressure control (SBP target generally <160 mmHg pre-securing), and cardiac telemetry
5. Monitor serum sodium closely for cerebral salt wasting/SIADH and avoid hypotonic fluids
6. Initiate daily transcranial Doppler monitoring days 4-14 post-bleed for vasospasm surveillance

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not perform lumbar puncture — CT has already confirmed SAH, and LP here adds herniation risk without diagnostic value
- Do not start aspirin, other antiplatelets, or NSAIDs for headache before the aneurysm is secured — raises rebleeding risk
- Do not administer nimodipine intravenously — oral or NG only, IV dosing has caused fatal cardiovascular collapse
- Do not pursue cardiac catheterization for the troponin elevation absent strong primary-ACS evidence — this would delay aneurysm-directed care
- Do not allow uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >160 mmHg) pre-securing, and avoid over-correcting to systemic hypotension
