### Primary Diagnosis
Guillain-Barré syndrome, cranial variant — isolated bilateral ptosis with global areflexia (Confidence: 0.68)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Myasthenia gravis — global areflexia and absent RNS decrement/normal single-fiber jitter argue strongly against NMJ transmission failure
2. Structural brainstem/cranial nerve III lesion — excluded by normal brainstem and cranial nerve MRI with no enhancement
3. Botulism — typically produces pupillary involvement and rapidly progressive bulbar/respiratory weakness, neither present here; areflexia less universal early

### Key Evidence
- Global areflexia with preserved strength and sensation on exam — the pivotal sign against MG, which spares reflexes
- Absent F-waves and H-reflex with demyelinating conduction changes from order_specialized_test — supports a proximal demyelinating radiculoneuropathy
- Normal RNS and normal single-fiber jitter from the same study — excludes NMJ decrement as the mechanism
- Albuminocytologic dissociation on analyze_csf — elevated protein with near-normal cell count, classic supportive finding for GBS-spectrum disease
- Normal brainstem/cranial nerve MRI — excludes a structural mimic

### Recommendations
1. Send confirmatory anti-GQ1b and broader ganglioside (GT1a, GM1) panel to characterize the cranial/Miller Fisher-spectrum variant
2. Perform bedside ice-pack test and AChR antibody assay to formally close out residual myasthenic possibility before committing fully to immunotherapy
3. Initiate IVIG (0.4 g/kg/day x 5 days) or plasma exchange as first-line disease-modifying therapy given the demyelinating pattern and albuminocytologic dissociation
4. Continue serial respiratory monitoring (FVC/NIF) given cranial GBS variants can progress to bulbar or respiratory involvement
5. Check drug interactions/contraindications (renal function, IgA deficiency) before administering IVIG, per protocol for any prescribing decision

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start corticosteroid monotherapy — it is not effective disease-modifying therapy in GBS and would delay IVIG/plasma exchange
- Do not treat empirically for myasthenia gravis (anticholinesterase or steroids) on the basis of ptosis alone when areflexia is present without supportive RNS/AChR evidence — this would delay appropriate GBS-directed therapy
- Do not skip serial respiratory function monitoring — cranial GBS variants can extend to bulbar and respiratory muscles despite a reassuring baseline FVC/NIF
