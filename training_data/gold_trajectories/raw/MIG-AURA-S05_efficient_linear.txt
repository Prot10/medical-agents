### Primary Diagnosis
Migraine with typical visual aura, new-onset (ICHD-3 1.2.1) (Confidence: 0.80)

### Differential Diagnoses
1. Occipital lobe seizure - aura here is slow, gradual (20 min march) and stereotyped across episodes, unlike the typically brief, rapid seconds-to-minutes evolution of an epileptic aura
2. Structural/vascular lesion (AVM, arterial dissection) - normal brain MRI and normal exam make this unlikely
3. Retinal/ophthalmic migraine - phenomenon is bilateral field/hemifield with cortical expanding pattern, not monocular, and fits a cortical spreading depression pattern better

### Key Evidence
- Gradual expanding scintillating scotoma over ~20 minutes followed by unilateral throbbing headache with photophobia/nausea, stereotyped across four episodes — meets ICHD-3 1.2 criteria for migraine with aura
- No SNOOP red flags on history or exam
- Normal brain MRI — excludes structural cause

### Recommendations
1. Sumatriptan 50-100 mg PO at headache onset (not during aura) for abortive treatment
2. Headache diary to track frequency, duration, and triggers
3. Trigger education: reduce screen strain (20-20-20 rule, blue-light filtering, ergonomic setup) and address sleep/stress given her new job
4. Counsel against combined oestrogen-progestogen contraception given elevated ischemic stroke risk with migraine with aura; discuss non-estrogen alternatives if contraception is needed
5. Reassess frequency/disability at follow-up before considering preventive therapy

### Red Flags / Alerts
- Do not start combined oestrogen-progestogen contraception in this patient given migraine with aura
- Do not co-administer triptans with ergotamine derivatives
- Return precautions: any change in aura character (new lateralization, motor/speech involvement, duration >60 min) warrants urgent re-evaluation
