\section*{Abstract (150 words, no references)}

\role{The whole paper in six moves; the reader must leave with the thesis and the central number.}

\content
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Thesis} (agreed 18 Nov 2026 and revised the same day after the first real results;
every figure, claim and section serves it). To support physicians in hospitals, a diagnostic agent
must reach the diagnosis through the work-up that clinical practice asks for. Rewarding a small open
model for following physician-reviewed case policies teaches it to order the tests physicians ask for
and few others, on conditions and real admissions it never trained on: it orders about as many tests
as physicians do and spends where the case needs it. Rewarding the same model for a correct diagnosis
at low cost keeps the diagnoses and saves by leaving required tests out.
The hospital is the goal, not a result: alignment with clinical practice is what the paper measures
(physician-reviewed policies, blind ratings by neurologists, the tests clinicians recorded).
\item \textbf{General claim for NMI readers.} An agent learns what its reward can see. An outcome
reward cannot tell a complete work-up from a guess that happens to be right, so it does not teach the
work-up; an executable specification that experts can audit does. Shown in one domain, with one
trained model: neurology is the testbed and the wider reach is an outlook.
\end{itemize}

\structure
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Constraint}: patient data cannot leave the hospital, so the agent is a 4--9\,B open model.
\item \textbf{Gap}: sequential-diagnosis benchmarks score the outcome (diagnosis, cost) and cannot tell a
complete work-up from an incomplete one that reaches the same answer; as a reward, the outcome pays
for leaving tests out.
\item \textbf{Idea}: one executable policy per case, reviewed by two neurologists, used both as the
measure and as the reinforcement-learning reward.
\item \textbf{Resource}: NeuroBench, 600 synthetic cases of 20 neurological conditions and 16 tools,
plus 100 recorded MIMIC-IV admissions for external testing.
\item \textbf{Central result}: Qwen3.5-9B trained on the policy reward against the same model trained on
diagnosis and cost, on four conditions never seen in training and on the MIMIC-IV admissions.
\item \textbf{Implication}: executable process specifications are a verifiable training signal wherever
experts write protocols.
\end{enumerate}
