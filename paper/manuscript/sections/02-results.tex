\section{Results (2,350 words, six subsections)}\label{sec:results}

\role{Turn the thesis into evidence in the order a sceptic needs it: trust the object (R1), see the gap
(R2), see the fix (R3), see that physicians agree (R4), see that it leaves the simulator (R5), see that a
small model is enough (R6).}

\noindent\textbf{Rules for every subsection.} Open with the claim as a sentence; one figure; effect sizes
with 95\% CIs, $n$ in cases, the test used; the case is the unit, seeds averaged within case; the
NeuroBench test carries the primary comparison and MIMIC-IV the external one, never pooled; method
details go to Methods.

\subsection{R1. An executable clinical-policy benchmark for local agents}\label{sec:benchmark}

\role{The reader must trust the object before any result trained on it; reviewers look here for leakage
and simulator artefacts.}

\content
\begin{itemize}
\item Episode: presentation, one typed action per turn (16 tools), stored results, a forced final
assessment.
\item Policy and score: required, recommended and avoided actions, order, accepted diagnoses; the policy
score from 0 to 1 with caps; perfect-agent check as proof of internal consistency.
\item Dataset: 600 synthetic cases of 20 conditions split by whole condition (419 / 60 / 119); review by
two neurologists and inter-physician agreement; 100 MIMIC-IV admissions for testing only.
\end{itemize}

(ED Fig.~1); agreement details (ED Fig.~3).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fig.~1} (overview): the cases, one real episode of the trained agent on a validation case,

\subsection{R3. Rewarding the work-up teaches it without losing diagnoses}\label{sec:rl}

\role{Claim C2: the causal test of the thesis. Everything else supports this subsection, so it gets the
most words.}

\content
\begin{itemize}
\item Design: two models trained from the same untrained Qwen3.5-9B with GRPO, differing only in the
reward: the policy score, or diagnosis and cost (0.85 and 0.15, as in SDBench). Same cases, seeds and
budget; each checkpoint chosen on validation by its own reward.
\item Test: the 119 cases of four conditions never seen in training, paired by case. Plain measures
of the work-up (correct diagnosis, required tests, unsafe actions, cost) and both scores, so each
model is judged also on the reward it was not trained on.
\item Mechanism: the outcome-reward model stops ordering tests; it keeps the diagnoses the history alone
gives and loses those that need tests.
\item Statistics: Wilcoxon and paired bootstrap; McNemar for proportions; Benjamini--Hochberg.
\end{itemize}

\structure
\begin{enumerate}
\item Design in two sentences, the two rewards (100 words).
\item What each model learns in training, Fig.~3a (100).
\item Main effect on the test cases, Fig.~3b and ED Table~1 (180).
\item Per condition, Fig.~3c (80).
\item Why the outcome reward loses diagnoses, Fig.~3d (100).
\item Cost and tokens (40).
\end{enumerate}

\artefacts
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fig.~3} (below). \textbf{ED Table~1}: every number with CIs and $P$.
\item \textbf{ED Fig.~2} reward history; \textbf{ED Fig.~4} other models; \textbf{ED Fig.~5} per
condition; \textbf{ED Fig.~6} acute vs chronic; \textbf{ED Fig.~8} robustness. \textbf{Supp.\
Tables~3--4} per condition, controls and ablations.
\end{itemize}
