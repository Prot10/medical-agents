## Source Case (Real Published Case Report)

**Source**: Journal of Medical Case Reports (PMCID: PMC8317402)
**Original diagnosis**: Anti-GABAB receptor encephalitis

### Clinical Presentation (from published case):
A 66-year-old man with a history of Gleason score 8 (4 + 4) prostate adenocarcinoma treated with radiotherapy and hormonal therapy presented in September 2017 after two episodes of generalized tonic–clonic seizures followed by postictal confusion. His prostate-specific antigen level at that time was 1.53 ng/mL. He reported new memory and concentration difficulties and a progressive depressive syndrome starting after the first seizure. 

On initial examination, vital signs were stable; neurologic examination was notable for mild confusion but no focal deficits. Standard laboratory studies, including complete blood count and metabolic panel, were unremarkable. Noncontrast head CT and brain MRI showed no acute abnormalities. 

Levetiracetam was started but soon discontinued owing to poor seizure control, and the patient was readmitted for recurrent generalized seizures. During hospitalization, he developed confusion, slurred speech, impaired comprehension, and focal seizures characterized by chewing movements, sniffing, rightward gaze deviation, and automatisms. 

An electroencephalogram demonstrated epileptiform discharges originating in the left frontotemporal region with secondary generalization. Intravenous clonazepam and levetiracetam led to clinical improvement, although interictal slow-wave foci persisted on follow-up EEG. 

Cerebrospinal fluid analysis revealed normal cell counts, glucose, and protein levels; bacterial cultures and herpes simplex virus PCR were negative. Oligoclonal immunoglobulin bands were present, and testing for anti-Hu, anti-Ri, and anti-Yo antibodies in the CSF was negative.

### Published Diagnostic Reasoning:
1. Infectious encephalitis was considered and excluded based on negative CSF bacterial culture and herpes PCR — “Bacterial culture and herpes virus polymerase chain reaction (PCR) in the CSF were both negative.”  
2. Metastatic brain disease was considered given his cancer history but was unlikely because “initial neurological examination, standard laboratory tests, and brain imaging [brain scanner and MRI] did not show any abnormalities.”  
3. Paraneoplastic limbic encephalitis associated with classical onconeural antibodies (anti-Hu, Ri, Yo) was less likely since there was an “absence of Hu, Ri, and Yo neural antibodies.”  
4. A paraneoplastic etiology related to small-cell lung carcinoma was suspected but no malignant cells were found on bronchoscopy with bronchial aspiration — “bronchoscopy with bronchial aspiration were performed, showing no evidence of malignancy.”  
5. Autoimmune limbic encephalitis due to GABAB receptor antibodies was supported by the finding that “GABA B anti-receptor antibodies were positive in the CSF, thus evoking the diagnosis of limbic encephalitis with GABA B receptor antibodies.”

  - Initial presentation mimics a different condition
