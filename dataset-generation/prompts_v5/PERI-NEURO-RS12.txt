## Source Case (Real Published Case Report)

**Source**: Radiology Case Reports (PMCID: PMC10692474)
**Original diagnosis**: Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy

### Clinical Presentation (from published case):
A 15-year-old boy with primary adrenal insufficiency due to a known hemizygous ABCD1 mutation was referred to neurology after an unrelated screening brain MRI showed T2 FLAIR hyperintense lesions in the cerebral peduncles without gadolinium enhancement. He had mild lower-extremity spasticity, nonsustained ankle clonus, and a mildly spastic gait (neurologic function scale 1). Neuropsychiatric testing revealed a full-scale IQ of 86 and a verbal comprehension index of 89.  
Given his evolving leg symptoms, an MRI of the entire spine with gadolinium was performed. This showed enhancement of the cauda equina nerve roots, predominantly at L1 and L3, with a normal spinal cord signal and anatomy of the conus medullaris.  
Two weeks later, he developed bilateral gastrocnemius pain, hypoesthesia and paresthesia in his right foot and left hand, and severely diminished patellar and Achilles reflexes. There was no cranial nerve involvement or respiratory compromise.  
Lumbar puncture results: total nucleated cell count 3/mm3 (normal 0–7), protein 65 mg/dL (normal 15–45), glucose 53 mg/dL (normal 40–70), negative culture and stain; CSF myelin basic protein 1.63 ng/mL (normal 0–5.5).  
Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy was initially suspected, and he received IVIG (2 g/kg over 5 days).  
EMG/NCS of the left upper and lower extremities revealed a length-dependent polyneuropathy with demyelinating features and multiple sensory and motor conduction blocks. He reported improvement in pain and was ambulatory after IVIG, but continued to have mild lower-extremity pain and a spastic gait.

### Published Diagnostic Reasoning:
1. AIDP was considered because of acute painful neuropathy with albuminocytologic dissociation and initial response to IVIG — “Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (AIDP) was suspected, so intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) … was given”  
2. AIDP was less likely as his nerve-root enhancement preceded symptom onset and persisted beyond 28 days — “the duration of cauda equina nerve root enhancement in AIDP … up to 28 days … our case had nerve root enhancement preceding the onset of his symptoms … and prolonged contrast enhancement beyond 28 days”  
3. Adrenomyeloneuropathy was excluded due to absence of spinal cord atrophy, young age, and preserved continence — “Our case is clinically and radiographically inconsistent with adrenomyeloneuropathy due to the absence of spinal cord atrophy, his young age of onset, and retained continence”  
4. Direct X-ALD nerve-root enhancement was unexpected, as it has not been previously reported — “Contrast enhancement of spinal nerves has not been reported in X-ALD”  
5. Metachromatic and globoid cell leukodystrophies can show similar root enhancement but were unlikely given his known ABCD1 mutation and lack of other features — “Cauda equina nerve root enhancement has previously been reported with metachromatic leukodystrophy and globoid cell leukodystrophy”  
6. CIDP became the favored diagnosis given persistent enhancement at 8 weeks and sustained improvement with serial IVIG — “persistent contrast enhancement after 8 weeks and improvement with IVIG indicates that he developed comorbid CIDP”
