## Source Case (Real Published Case Report)

**Source**: Radiology Case Reports (PMCID: PMC10432142)
**Original diagnosis**: Atypical meningioma

### Clinical Presentation (from published case):
A 44-year-old woman with known breast cancer on chemotherapy underwent a routine Tc-99m MDP bone scan, which showed patchy increased uptake over the right frontal and right fronto-parietal skull on SPECT imaging. She denied headache, seizures, loss of consciousness, nausea, vomiting, fever, or weakness. Neurological examination was unremarkable. MRI of the brain demonstrated a well-defined extra-axial mass overlying the right frontal lobe that was iso-intense to gray matter on T1 and T2 sequences, showed no diffusion restriction in DWI and ADC sequences, and enhanced avidly after intravenous gadolinium administration, with surrounding edema.

### Published Diagnostic Reasoning:
1. Bone metastasis was initially suspected because of her breast‐cancer history — “Due to the patient's status, metastasis was suspected, and an MRI scan was requested to confirm the findings.”  
2. Cerebral infarction was considered among causes of tracer uptake in acute tissue necrosis but was excluded because “no diffusion restriction in DWI (B) and ADC (C) sequences with avid enhancement in T1 postcontrast (E).”  
3. Calcified aneurysm was a possible source of atypical tracer uptake but was deemed unlikely given the imaging demonstrated a discrete extra-axial mass rather than a vascular lesion — “The cranial uptake of a radioisotope can be seen in entities other than meningiomas such as tumoral calcinosis, infarction, and calcified aneurysm.”  
4. Tumoral calcinosis was included in the differential for nonosseous uptake but was excluded because MRI revealed a soft-tissue mass without features of calcific deposits — “The cranial uptake of a radioisotope can be seen in entities other than meningiomas such as tumoral calcinosis, infarction, and calcified aneurysm.”  
5. Meningioma was favored given an enhancing extra-axial lesion in a typical location and known mechanisms of radionuclide uptake in meningiomas, including calcification and reactive bone changes — “In meningiomas, bone-seeking radionuclides are taken up in response to 3 known factors affecting calcium deposition, which are: tumor calcification, calvarial erosion, and reactive bone formation.”
